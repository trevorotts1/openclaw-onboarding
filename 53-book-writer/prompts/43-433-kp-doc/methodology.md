## Method — how to write the KP document

Write a flowing prose document with the following structure, in order. Use
headings where they help, but the body must read as a coherent case.

1. **The system in one breath.** Open by naming the program and stating its
   central promise in a single sentence: who it is for, the method, and the
   outcome. This is the "short message" the whole offer hangs on.
2. **Name the problem.** Make the reader's problem specific and personal. Say who
   the reader is (the ideal avatar from the dossier), what they were before, what
   they are now, and the exact feeling that marks the problem — the gap between
   their old skill and their new job.
3. **The cost of inaction.** Show what the problem quietly costs — the habits that
   used to make them strong now make them the bottleneck; work waits on them;
   the team brings them problems instead of solutions. End the section on the
   single line that names the wound.
4. **The system in four phases.** Present the FOUR phases in order. For each
   phase:
   - Name the phase.
   - State the phase's outcome (its Transformational Outcome).
   - Name the three chapters that carry the phase (chapters 1-3, 4-6, 7-9, 10-12),
     with a one-line description of the work of each chapter.
   - Explain the phase's job in the overall transformation.
   The four phases must read as a progression, and each phase must clearly map to
   exactly three chapters of the book.
5. **The through-line.** Close by naming the one trade or idea that runs through
   the whole system, restating what the reader becomes by the end, and ending on
   a single memorable line that leaves the promise hanging.

The golden example of this document is approximately 400-600 words. Match that
density: specific, warm, and persuasive. Do not pad.
