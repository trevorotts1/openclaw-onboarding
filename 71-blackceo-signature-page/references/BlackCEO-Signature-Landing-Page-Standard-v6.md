Every applicable section-level criterion must reach at least 8/10, and no hard requirement may fail. An overall score cannot conceal a weak solution, vague inclusion or unfinished founder letter. Apply the specific emotional tests already taught below; repair the actual weakness, not the client's intent. Claim verification and approval remain the human reviewer's responsibility, not an additional AI claim-audit task.

An existing stricter repository repeated-failure trigger is honored earlier. If it remains blocked, identify the exact issue and escalate to the configured lead, not an automatic human approval queue. Never forward a failed item, rewrite passing sections unnecessarily, or keep reviewing an unchanged pass three times. Recheck repaired passages and affected transitions/counts only.

### C. Copy, fonts and images stay coordinated

Complete and QC the copy before full wireframes. Establish Google Fonts, or selected Google Fonts equivalents, and the font map before the full wireframes; check actual font use during rendering and later HTML font loading in the browser. Do not build a separate GHL website just to choose fonts. Actual client brand artwork remains artwork, not recreated text.

One selected page normally needs desktop and mobile wireframes; four wireframes apply only when producing both standard and long-form template sets. The image inventory determines image count. Each individual image prompt is 5,000-20,000 characters, governed by the linked image guide and its explicit current-runtime compatibility notes. No Midjourney workflow is used.

### D. Only the corrected, QC-passed version advances

The downstream designer/generator/coder receives the current passed copy and assets, never an earlier rejected draft. Changes authorized after layout trigger checks only of the affected work. The shared SOP supplies this handoff rule and its do-not-repeat checklist. This revision creates no live GHL changes or repository deployment.

## Writing standard: make the reader feel the meaning

The page is one emotional journey, not twelve unrelated advertisements. Its opening promises a desirable future. Its pains make the reader feel understood. Its responses make change imaginable. Its Why declares the commitment. Its Who establishes recognition. Its What provides clarity. Its Benefits make that clarity personally meaningful. Its How To makes participation tangible. Its founder letter turns the invitation into a human connection.

**The destination must be larger than the first exercise.** A calendar exercise is a method; being included in your own life is a reason. A handoff template is a tool; freedom to lead and be present is a reason. A writing prompt is a beginning; giving a meaningful story your own voice is a reason. Keep method and meaning connected, not interchangeable.

### Write the inner meaning, not just the visible problem

A scene works when the detail reveals a cost, desire, or conflict. A dinner table alone is decoration. Nodding while missing the conversation reveals absence. Smiling after saying yes while closing an untouched notebook reveals self-erasure. Editing away the one detail that made a memory yours reveals the pressure to perform. Use the client's situations and vocabulary, not these same props on every page.

A forceful declaration can carry equal weight: the business gets your response all day and your leadership only when there is something left. Long description is not the only route to emotional recognition. Choose the form that reaches the experience most directly.

**The problem is the villain; the reader's dignity is not.** Let the copy confront the pattern, speak to the desire, and invite the decision. Emotional intensity does not require insulting the reader or making them perform distress.

### Change the emotional register as the page moves

The Big Bold Claim should have conviction and scale. Pain should feel close and recognizable. The matched solution releases tension. The Why takes a stand. The What is concrete and desirable. The Benefits let the reader picture the payoff. The How To is clear and encouraging. The founder letter becomes intimate, then rises into a personal invitation. Do not write the whole page in one volume or one sentence pattern.

Use direct address, contrast, purposeful repetition, and original language when they earn their place. Words such as freedom, power, purpose, and possibility are welcome when the copy gives them meaning. All capitals, exclamation marks, and grand adjectives cannot substitute for a meaningful thought. Equally, do not drain a strong thought by automatically shrinking every promise into a tiny step.

### Voice belongs to this founder and audience

Use the client's supplied speech, terms, convictions, and lived material. Match how this person would actually address these readers. Cultural expressions, faith language, profanity, and familiar forms of address depend on the brief; none is automatic merely because this is BlackCEO. Do not make every client sound like the same motivational coach.

The earlier references to Les Brown, Simon Sinek, and Brene Brown describe broad requested qualities: motivational conviction in the opening, purpose-led reasons in the What and Benefits, and vulnerability in the letter. Use those qualities in original BlackCEO copy, not anyone's distinctive phrasing, signature lines, or identifiable imitation.

### Repetition must add meaning

A deliberate callback changes the reader's relationship to a detail. Pain: dinner passes while the owner answers messages. Benefit: the owner hears the whole story. Founder letter: the founder admits that being needed helped keep the pattern alive. These are three functions, not three interchangeable paragraphs about a phone.

The three solutions, the inclusion list, and the three benefits must remain distinct. Solutions answer particular pains. The What tells exactly what is included. Benefits explain why the results matter in the reader's life. A method can recur where needed; a paragraph cannot merely be copied into all three locations.

### Length must concentrate meaning, not ration care

In the standard version, work within the original compact limits by selecting sharper details and cutting filler. Do not quietly lift those limits. In the long form, develop the scene and reasoning as much as the section needs within its suggested range. Do not turn every panel into an essay or pad it to the upper target. Long-form targets are guidance; the source's explicit standard limits remain requirements.

The detailed examples show three consistent fictional page concepts, not three scripts to recycle. Learn the writing move, then use the real brief.

## Copy choreography and phone-width reading

### What the supplied pages add to the writing instruction

The Elevation JPEG and the two BlackCEO Launch Challenge screenshots are copy-and-layout references, not a replacement for this guide's structure. The screenshots are low-resolution: use their legible display copy and visible arrangement, not an invented transcription of the small paragraphs. The challenge image beginning with the event title precedes the continuation image containing Day 5 and the closing founder letter.

The challenge uses direct, spoken statements; contrasts between the present struggle and the desired change; benefit-led names for curriculum; large chapter markers; selective emphasis inside headlines; and a shift from business outcomes to personal meaning. The readable line "WRITES YOUR AD COPY FOR YOU" is followed by "NO MORE STARING AT A BLANK SCREEN!" This is a useful writing move: name what the offering does, then translate it into a recognizable experience the reader wants to stop having. "FROM ONE ENTREPRENEUR TO ANOTHER" changes the relationship at the letter, moving from presentation to personal address. These observations come from the supplied screenshots, not an outside analysis of conversion performance.

Version 4 already teaches conviction, inner conflict, a rising Why, and the founder's comeback. Keep that work. The addition here is **how those words become a sequence of readable visual moments without losing their force or meaning**. A strong page needs both an emotional progression and a readable display progression.

### Communication moves to preserve

**Lead with the consequence that matters.** A named tool is not the entire promise. Translate the work into the experience the reader can recognize. Do not replace the actual inclusion with a slogan; let the slogan introduce a specific explanation.

**Let the main sentence land before explaining it.** A short, forceful declaration can sit alone. The following paragraph develops the point rather than repeating it. Do not spread a dependent sentence over different panels merely to create more screens.

**Use emphasis as meaning, not decoration.** Identify the phrase that carries the contrast or payoff. Make that phrase larger, heavier, or a separate line in the handoff. Do not make every word equally loud, and do not rely on color to supply a missing argument. Do not require bold type on every sentence.

**Let aspiration and intimacy coexist.** The references use commanding portraits, formal clothing, close crops, and image-led moments as well as vulnerable language. The picture need not portray a defeated person every time the copy names a pain. Its role may be recognition, aspiration, presence, or a transition. Specify the role; do not infer a person's biography from a portrait.

**Treat chapter markers as navigation through an experience.** The repeated Day banners in the challenge announce a curriculum progression. They do not create a rule that every new page needs a five-day event, timers, repeated offers, new guarantees, or those same headings. Adapt the move only when the client's actual offering has that structure. Preserve the current CTA map.

**Move from practical gain into personal meaning.** The challenge's business-to-life transition is a model for connecting an inclusion to why the reader cares. The Benefits should not simply repeat the What with bigger adjectives. The founder's letter should become a conversation, not another row of marketing tiles.

Keep phrases intact when possible, allow sensible rewrapping on a narrow screen, and retain every word. Do not expand the standard section beyond its limit to accommodate a visual treatment. Count all new public text if the client selects one of these fragments for a real page.

### Three separate identifiers: section, visual panel, exported part

A **framework section** has a persuasive job. A **visual panel** is a reading unit within it. An **export part** is a slice of a tall wireframe delivered as an image. They must not be confused.

Use `Section 2 / P01` and `Section 2 / P02` for two visual panels inside Pain 1. Both remain Section 2. Do not call the second pain panel `2A`: that identifier belongs to Solution 1 in the long form. Likewise use `Section 7 / P01`, `P02`, and so on for layout panels. Existing 5A/5B/5C content subdivisions remain children of Section 5, and they can each have their own P01/P02 visual subdivisions if needed.

Part A, Part B, and later exported PNGs are consecutive slices of one phone-width page. The next file continues from the previous file's final section or panel. They are not side-by-side phone screens, alternative layouts, new top-level sections, or permission to restart the page. Every included sentence appears once and in the correct reading order.

### When and how to break up a section

Choose reading breaks after drafting enough real copy to know its weight, before fixing the final wireframe height. Then check the copy in the chosen width and type size. The process is **write -> group by meaning -> assign type/image roles -> wrap at phone width -> extend or divide the visual treatment -> check continuity**. Do not fill a preselected rectangular box by shrinking type or deleting the paragraph that contains the emotional point.

Begin a new visual panel when the thought changes, when a long paragraph sequence needs a meaningful pause, when an inclusion group finishes, or when the founder story moves to a new narrative part. A panel split is not mandatory at a fixed word count. In the compact standard, many pains need only one panel. In the long form, the same function may need two or more panels.

Prefer paragraph boundaries. Avoid isolating a heading from the paragraph it introduces, separating a question from its answer, placing an image between the two halves of a contrast, or cutting a CTA reason away from its button. Keep the last sentence of one panel and the first of the next connected. Images can provide pauses without adding more public copy. A highlighted sentence moved into a display treatment stays in its original reading position; do not count it twice or repeat it as filler.

When a page is reflowed for mobile, **retain the full writing**. The number of screens may increase. Do not silently turn the long form into the standard version, replace the founder letter with a summary, or hide the inclusions behind a carousel or accordion merely to reduce height.

### Mobile typography and geometry: an implementation baseline

These values are a design baseline for the accompanying corrected wireframes, not quotations from the historical pages or requirements for every possible device. Use **CSS pixels** for web layout and **points** for the separate editable-document rule. A 2x PNG doubles raster dimensions; it does not double the intended on-phone font size.

| Element | Baseline at a 390 CSS-pixel page width |
|---|---|
| Content column | One column; 24-pixel side margins; 342-pixel body width |
| Big opening claim | 40-pixel display type / 44-pixel line height, with a secondary emphasis phrase at 28/36 where useful |
| Major section headline | 32/38; keep complete thought units readable rather than forcing desktop line breaks |
| Inclusion or persona title | 22/30; the descriptive body remains separate |
| Normal body and founder letter | 18/28; full-width paragraphs, never a narrow column beside a tall portrait |
| Supporting line | 18/28 or 20/30 according to length |
| Display emphasis within a section | 24/34 to 30/37; use selectively, not for the entire long letter |
| Button wording | 18-pixel bold; full-width target at least 56 pixels high, growing for a long label |
| Public microcopy | 16/24; do not make the click clarification unreadably small |
| Internal wireframe annotations | 14/20; not audience-facing website copy |

Test wrapping at 320, 360, 390, and 430 CSS-pixel widths. A static image documents one layout; it is not proof of device testing or of an accessible implemented website. Let the eventual live page reflow when text is enlarged. Never disable zoom to preserve a screenshot's composition.

This reflow direction is informed by W3C's explanation of readable vertical content at 320 CSS pixels. The 56-pixel button baseline is this design's choice and exceeds the 44-by-44 CSS-pixel enhanced target-size criterion; it is not a claim that 56 pixels is the mandatory WCAG minimum. Public reference details are at the end of this guide. The 12-point minimum in Google Docs or an explicitly requested Word document remains unchanged.

### Image sizing and visual continuity

Use real frame proportions: a 2:3 frame must have height equal to width multiplied by 3/2; a 9:16 frame uses 16/9; a 16:9 frame uses 9/16. Do not label a square as a vertical image. For example, the corrected 390-pixel layouts use a 342 x 192.375 landscape frame, a 228 x 342 portrait, and a 168 x 298.667 narrow portrait. Their labels describe geometry, not finished photography.

Choose an image's crop for its job. A landscape environmental frame can bridge ideas; a portrait can carry presence; a tall detail can give an intentional pause. Offset a smaller image left or right when useful, but place long text above or below it instead of squeezing the paragraph into the leftover strip. Keep the image within the page width. Do not add an image to every panel just to prove variety.

Visual continuity comes from a repeated margin system, a small type scale, consistent line spacing, repeated rules or framing, one CTA treatment, and content that continues logically. Change image proportion, emphasis, and light/dark treatment intentionally. Avoid a random new component for every paragraph. Alternation should follow the emotional movement, not a rigid demand to invert every section.

### Special handling for copy-heavy areas

**Sections 2, 3, and 4:** In the long form, a first panel can establish the lived moment and a second can reveal its private cost. Keep the complete pain before its matching solution. This is layout editing, not permission to add question-led pain copy: the original second-person statement rule still governs even where the historical challenge uses questions.

**Section 5:** Keep the recognition, commitment, and invitation connected. Use the existing 5A/5B/5C option only when needed. Do not squeeze three fully developed paragraphs into three mobile columns. The family's one CTA follows its final child.

**Section 7:** Write the complete overview and five to ten inclusions. For mobile, show each inclusion at full readable width, with its title and actual explanation. Group related items in consecutive panels, allowing an image or typographic pause between groups. Every inclusion remains visible; the long-form CTA comes after the entire family. The standard still has no added Section 7 button.

**Section 11:** Keep all seven steps in a vertical sequence with readable text. A thin rail or repeated step number can provide continuity. Do not put seven steps across a phone screen, and do not replace their instructions with seven generic icons. Steps 1-3 and 4-7 can form two reading groups without restarting the count. Only the long form adds the separate concluding button.

**Section 12:** Preserve the actual letter and all six internal parts. One portrait can sit between complete paragraphs. ## Section 1: The Big Bold Claim

**Reader response to create:** I want that future. I want to know how this offering helps me reach it.

**Length and CTA rule:** 180-225 visible characters, including the offer name and CTA under this guide's counting convention. Approximately 30-45 words may fit, but characters control. A strong short claim is concentrated, not emotionally small.

### How to write this section

Write a declaration of the major desired future, not an explanation of the first exercise. Make the promise the dominant reading. Name the offer in the headline or a supporting line and connect it to the promise. Include the opening outcome-led CTA. The name may be displayed as an eyebrow, but the promise should command attention.

A complaint is not the opening promise. A statement that the reader deserves more is not enough until the copy shows what more means. A practical tool can support the claim, but must not reduce its emotional scale. A brief contrast is useful only when the desirable future remains the center of gravity.

Above the fold is the visitor's initial view before scrolling. Supply a design direction that keeps the promise, offer identification, and first CTA together. Font size, image height, and the actual screen determine the implemented fold; the copywriter supplies the intent, not a claim to have built or tested the website. ## Section 12: The Big Bold Heartfelt Message

**Reader response to create:** I understand the person inviting me, I recognize something of my own experience, and I feel a reason to take this next step.

**Length and CTA rule:** 100-150 visible words total, including sign-off. Part 1: 20-25 words. Keep all six internal labels. No extra standalone CTA button.

### How to write this section

Write a personal letter from the founder, with all six internal part labels. Keep it conversational and connected across those labels. The Struggle gets the most emotional attention; the close must rise beyond the struggle into a compelling invitation.

Use a supplied founder moment, an uncomfortable admission, a consequential decision, and a visible change in behavior or experience. Vulnerability is not the adjective vulnerable. It is the distance between what the founder showed others and what they privately knew or felt. Do not borrow a teaching founder's history for a real client.

The comeback needs to be visible. A kept commitment, a responsibility actually handed over, or a voice recognized again can carry it. Do not end the story at I began trying. Let the reader see what changed and why the founder cares about helping them make their own decision.

The Invite names the same offer and participation action as Section 1. The Passionate Close returns to the meaning of the opening promise through the founder's lived perspective. Bring back one meaningful image or unresolved desire with a new possibility. Finish with a direct invitation and the founder's signature. Do not bolt an unrelated motivational speech onto the last paragraph.

This is the personal, extended invitation at the end of the page, not an unrelated biography, an inventory of achievements, or an added standalone button module.

### The six-part letter: what each movement must accomplish

| Required internal part | Its job | Concrete sign it is present |
|---|---|---|
| Part 1 - The Big Bold Heartfelt Message | Open a personal conversation, not an institutional greeting | The founder has a reason for speaking directly to this reader |
| Part 2 - The Big Struggle | Reveal the founder's lived struggle and connection to the reader | A supplied moment and an uncomfortable private admission; I used to be just like you or a natural equivalent where it fits |
| Part 3 - The Big Decision | Make the turning point consequential and show the comeback beginning | A choice expressed in changed action or experience, with significance for the reader's own decision |
| Part 4 - The Big Reason | Turn the experience into a commitment to serve this audience | The offering exists for a human reason, not merely because the founder launched a business |
| Part 5 - The Big Invite | Invite the same offering and participation action named in Section 1 | A direct, personal invitation and a reason to accept it |
| Part 6 - The Big Passionate Close | Rise into the reader's possibility and finish with the founder's signature | The final thought recovers something the struggle put at risk and makes the next step meaningful |

The emotional center remains the Struggle. The Decision and Close must not be sacrificed to it. The reader should leave with a possibility and an invitation, not simply an account of what hurt the founder.


**Compact allocation:** Keep the 100-150-word total and the original 20-25-word opening. As an editorial aid, allow roughly 30-40 words for the Struggle and use the remaining room for decision, reason, invitation, close, and signature. The six movements still have to work; the close cannot be replaced by a generic thank-you.

**Observable writing test:** Locate the specific founder moment, private admission, consequential decision, visible comeback, personal offer invitation, and rising final thought. The full six-part letter must move through all of them; do not call a polite sign-off a passionate close.

**DESIGN NOTE - NOT FOR PUBLICATION:** Present as a personal letter. Preserve all six labels in the handoff but let the prose connect naturally. Use the actual founder signature. A contextual link is allowed; another standalone button is not mandated.

## Emotional quality review: point to the words, then repair the weak part

This is a writing review, not an audit of client claims. Correct section labels and counts cannot compensate for emotionally flat copy. Equally, powerful sentences do not authorize changing the framework. The checks below turn the intended quality into questions about the actual draft.

| Check | What must be visible in the copy | Competent-but-flat warning | Focused repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening promise | A major desirable future, the named offer, and an opening CTA | The hero explains a worksheet or starts with pain | Write the destination first; place the method beneath it |
| Felt recognition | A behavior, scene, or inner contradiction plus its private significance | The pain only says overwhelmed, frustrated, or stuck | Choose the moment that makes the feeling recognizable |
| Matching response | The specific preceding pain is answered by a relevant part of the offer | Each solution could follow any of the three pains | Connect one difficulty to one response and one changed moment |
| Conviction in the Why | A stand for the audience, a reason the offering exists, and an earned invitation | The section only announces a useful program | Name what the founder is committed to helping this reader reclaim or pursue |
| Clarity in the What | Identifiable inclusions and their immediate use | Motivational nouns replace the deliverables, or the list is a dry inventory | Pair a compelling title with a concrete inclusion and use |
| Human significance in Benefits | Three distinct payoffs, pictured in life rather than just explained | The benefits repeat the tools, repeat the pains, or repeat each other | Write what changes, why it matters, and the moment that makes it felt |
| Founder vulnerability and rise | Supplied scene, private admission, decision, visible comeback, invitation, and meaningful close | Generic struggle, an unexplained turnaround, or a polite ending | Restore the missing movement; let the ending recover something the struggle put at risk |
| Reason to act | The nearby copy explains why this click matters; the destination/action stays clear | A button could be transplanted anywhere without changing its meaning | Complete the section's emotional thought before inviting the action |
| Voice and continuity | Audience-specific language, changing emotional register, and a coherent offer | Every section uses the same cadence, props, or interchangeable slogans | Use the client's voice and make each return to a theme add something new |

Do not apply every test mechanically to every paragraph. A registration step needs clarity; it does not need a private confession. A compact claim needs a destination; it does not need a long pain scene. The section-specific test below each writing lesson is the local test.

**Within the initial QC, inspect both the dominant lines and the full copy.** Check whether the page moves coherently from promise to invitation, then identify the emotional and practical work each section adds. These are two views within one review, not mandatory repeated QC cycles. For a failing section, note one exact line that misses, the missing effect, and the smallest useful rewrite. Revise that passage and recheck its immediate neighbors and count. Passing sections do not receive unnecessary revisions.

Apply the shared SOP's failed-only three-attempt repair rule. Every applicable section criterion must reach 8 or higher, with no hard failure. A passing section advances immediately; do not revise or review it three more times. Repair a failed section, recheck its specific defects, immediate transitions and count, and stop when it passes. After up to three unsuccessful repair/recheck attempts, identify the blocker and escalate rather than forwarding weak work or changing the instructions. Missing client input remains internal; this is not a routine human approval gate.

### What a 10-level target means here

A top-level draft follows the brief, has a compelling opening, makes recognition and desired change felt, supplies concrete clarity, and carries the founder's vulnerability into an earned invitation. The language sounds chosen for this audience, not assembled from marketing defaults. Its strongest moments are not isolated; the quality holds through the whole page.

A rating is an editorial judgment, not a measured conversion rate or a promise that every model will perform identically. Do not print a self-rating in the client handoff. Deliver the actual copy and the requested internal implementation notes.
