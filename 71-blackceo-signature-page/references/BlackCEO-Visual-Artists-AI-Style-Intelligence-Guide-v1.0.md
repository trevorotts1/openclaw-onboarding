# 0. HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT

This document is not a list of adjectives. It is a research translation system.

Each entry is tied to a **human research reference** so a human can understand the lineage that was studied. That line is metadata only and must never be copied into an image-generation request. The executable system receives a new **branded style name**. When an AI image agent creates an image, it must use the branded system name and the operational rules - never the research-reference artist name. Before sending a prompt downstream, strip all human research-reference names and all source-work titles.

## Two execution modes

### A. Historical-reference synthesis
For deceased artists, the guide can follow the broad historical visual grammar more directly: composition, palette, mark-making, light, spatial logic, material behavior, narrative structure, and recurring formal devices. It must still avoid recreating a specific existing artwork, exact composition, or protected source image.

### B. Contemporary independent synthesis
For living artists, the guide extracts broad formal principles and recombines them into an original visual system. The agent must not reproduce signature characters, exact recurring motifs, famous compositions, or a one-to-one imitation. The objective is to preserve useful visual mechanics while producing independent work.

## Universal prompt assembly

Build prompts in this order:

1. **STYLE SYSTEM** - branded system name from this guide.
2. **SUBJECT** - who or what appears.
3. **SETTING** - physical, historical, environmental, or abstract context.
4. **COMPOSITION** - framing, scale, balance, negative space, viewpoint.
5. **MATERIAL / MARK LOGIC** - how the image should look physically made.
6. **PALETTE** - dominant hues, saturation, temperature, contrast.
7. **LIGHT / VALUE** - direction, softness, contrast, atmosphere.
8. **CONCEPT ENGINE** - symbolism, contradiction, story, or emotional objective.
9. **QUALITY GATE** - verify the system before rendering.
10. **AVOID** - prevent generic drift and accidental copying.

## Universal quality rule

Before rendering, score the draft concept from 1-10 on the categories specified in the selected system. **Any required category below 8 triggers a redesign before generation.** Attractive rendering does not compensate for weak style logic.

## Universal originality rule

Never reproduce a known artwork, exact pose, exact layout, signature character, famous prop arrangement, or recognizable source image. Use the visual grammar to create a new composition for the user's subject.

# 1. LEGACY STREET PORTRAIT
**Human research reference - DO NOT SEND TO IMAGE MODEL:** Jamel Shabazz  
**Execution mode:** Contemporary independent synthesis. Do not use the research-reference name in generation prompts.

## Research synthesis
Shabazz is repeatedly described as a documentarian of community, especially New York street life, hip-hop culture, fashion, youth identity, and neighborhood presence from the late 1970s and 1980s onward. Representative photographs are not merely fashion records. Their power comes from the combination of direct portraiture, social geography, personal dignity, recognizable period style, and the subject's active participation in how they are seen. Many images are eye-level, human-scale, and geographically specific. Clothing, jewelry, sneakers, cars, storefronts, stoops, subway architecture, brick, signage, and other street details become social evidence rather than decorative background.

The durable visual lesson is **relationship plus place**. The camera is close enough to communicate respect and personality but wide enough to preserve neighborhood information. Subjects often look directly at the camera or pose knowingly. The picture says: this person belongs to this place, at this moment, and chose how to present themselves.

## Visual DNA
- **Framing:** eye-level environmental portrait; medium-full to full body is often more useful than a tight headshot because fashion and stance carry meaning.
- **Subject behavior:** confident, self-aware, relaxed, socially connected; direct gaze is strong but not mandatory.
- **Pose language:** hands in pockets, crossed arms, slight lean, shoulder-to-shoulder grouping, stance organized around clothing and attitude rather than formal studio posing.
- **Setting:** real street architecture; stoops, sidewalks, brick, storefronts, transit, fences, parked cars, playgrounds, hand-painted signs, concrete.
- **Palette:** documentary-natural color. Preserve the period or local color of clothing and environment rather than imposing a heavy cinematic grade.
- **Light:** available daylight or simple direct flash behavior. Favor readable faces and clothing over theatrical shadow effects.
- **Lens logic:** normal to moderately wide perspective, physically near the subject; avoid long-lens paparazzi compression.
- **Texture:** film-like grain can be present but must not become a nostalgia filter.
- **Hierarchy:** person first, style second, environment third; all three remain legible.
- **Narrative:** the image should feel encountered in lived public space, not manufactured on a soundstage.

## AI preflight
Before rendering, answer:
1. What does the subject want the viewer to understand about them before any caption is read?
2. What two environmental details prove where this person is socially and physically located?
3. What clothing, jewelry, hair, footwear, or accessory choice communicates identity without caricature?
4. Is the camera position respectful and participatory rather than voyeuristic?
5. Would the image still feel specific if all artificial color grading were removed?

## Generation instructions
Create a grounded street portrait in which the subject controls their presence. Keep the camera at human eye level, close enough for emotional connection but wide enough to preserve body language, clothing, and neighborhood context. Use believable urban daylight, natural skin rendering, real street textures, and a documentary sense of timing. Let wardrobe and stance carry cultural information. Keep architecture recognizable and imperfect. Preserve small contextual evidence - storefront lettering, curb wear, transit details, old masonry, sidewalk seams - without cluttering the frame.

If a group is shown, make relationships visible through spacing and gesture rather than staged symmetry. If period specificity is requested, build it through accurate clothing silhouette, materials, street objects, vehicle era, signage, and grooming rather than a generic retro filter.

## Avoid
- fashion-editorial lighting that removes the documentary quality
- anonymous cyberpunk streets or generic graffiti backdrops
- exaggerated swagger, caricature, or costume-like hip-hop shorthand
- shallow depth of field that erases neighborhood context
- sepia nostalgia as a substitute for period accuracy
- voyeuristic candid framing when a direct, collaborative portrait would be stronger
- copying a known photograph, pose, location, or outfit combination

## Internal quality gate - target 8+
Score: subject dignity; environmental specificity; wardrobe authenticity; pose naturalism; documentary light; eye-level relationship; period accuracy when applicable; narrative specificity; originality. Any score below 8 requires revision.

## Research anchors
Vogue features and interviews on Shabazz's documentation of New York community, street style, and hip-hop culture; representative archival street portraits and exhibitions of *Back in the Days* and related bodies of work.

# 2. CODED CROWN EXPRESSION
**Human research reference - DO NOT SEND TO IMAGE MODEL:** Jean-Michel Basquiat  
**Execution mode:** Historical-reference synthesis. Never recreate a known painting, character arrangement, or exact inscription.

## Research synthesis
Museum descriptions emphasize Basquiat's merger of graffiti-era energy, drawing, painting, language, anatomy, symbols, Black history, jazz-like rhythm, street notation, and art-historical references. Works often combine raw figuration with written fragments, diagrammatic marks, repeated symbols, crossed-out words, crowns or halos, bones, skull-like heads, numerical fragments, arrows, and improvised surfaces. The apparent spontaneity is not random. Meaning is built through **layering, recurrence, cancellation, and collision**.

A key mechanism is that text behaves as image. Words may be repeated, partially obscured, crossed out, misspelled, abbreviated, boxed, or positioned as labels. Figures frequently appear mask-like or skeletal, with exposed teeth, organs, or structural anatomy. Backgrounds are active fields rather than passive color. Erasure remains visible. The work feels revised in public.

## Visual DNA
- **Composition:** dense but hierarchically unstable; central heads or figures may anchor a field of marks that competes for attention.
- **Line:** urgent, uneven, childlike only on the surface; alternate between scratchy contour, blunt block line, diagram line, and scribble.
- **Text:** short words, fragments, numbers, lists, labels, crossed-out phrases. Text should carry conceptual association, not decorative nonsense.
- **Symbols:** crowns, halos, bones, arrows, boxes, teeth, skeletal anatomy, diagrams, repeated marks. Do not use all at once.
- **Surface:** visible revisions, overpainting, exposed ground, scrawls over painted areas, color blocked and then interrupted.
- **Palette:** bone white, black, red, yellow, blue, ochre, raw brown, green; often high contrast and deliberately unrefined.
- **Figure:** frontal, mask-like, psychologically charged, disproportionate; anatomy can become diagram or emblem.
- **Rhythm:** syncopated. Repetition should feel musical: phrase, interruption, response, return.
- **Depth:** mostly shallow; marks sit on and against the surface rather than opening illusionistic space.
- **Emotion:** urgency, coded intelligence, confrontation, vulnerability, historical pressure.

## AI preflight
1. What is the dominant human or cultural idea?
2. What 3-5 textual fragments genuinely relate to that idea?
3. Which 2-3 symbols will recur and evolve?
4. Where will revision remain visible?
5. What will be deliberately left incomplete?

## Generation instructions
Build the image as a layered working surface rather than a finished illustration. Establish one dominant figure, head, object, or emblem. Surround it with a limited vocabulary of words, numeric fragments, arrows, diagrams, and symbolic marks connected to the subject. Allow some marks to be overwritten and some words to be crossed out while remaining readable underneath. Use flat color blocks and raw exposed ground. Break clean contour with secondary scratches and revisions. Let the main figure shift between portrait, mask, anatomy chart, and icon.

Text must be semantically related to the subject. For example, a prompt about ambition might generate vocabulary around price, ascent, ownership, labor, inheritance, time, or rank - not random pseudo-graffiti. Build three passes: **statement**, **collision**, **revision**. The final image should preserve evidence of all three.

## Avoid
- polished vector graffiti
- one clean cartoon figure on a blank background
- random illegible writing used only as texture
- smooth digital gradients or photorealistic rendering
- copying a known crown placement, skull, painting layout, or phrase
- using every famous symbol at once
- making the surface chaotic without a conceptual center

## Internal quality gate - target 8+
Score: layered surface logic; meaningful text-image integration; raw line energy; symbol coherence; figure intensity; visible revision; color tension; syncopated rhythm; originality. Any score below 8 requires another construction pass.

## Research anchors
Whitney Museum of American Art and Metropolitan Museum of Art collection essays describing Basquiat's symbolic vocabulary, language, anatomy, abstraction/figuration, wordplay, and layered street-derived mark systems.

# 3. REGAL PATTERN PORTRAITURE
**Human research reference - DO NOT SEND TO IMAGE MODEL:** Kehinde Wiley  
**Execution mode:** Contemporary independent synthesis. Preserve broad portrait mechanics but do not copy known poses, floral designs, paintings, or specific Old Master substitutions.

## Research synthesis
Museum sources repeatedly identify several durable features: highly finished realistic portraiture; contemporary Black sitters; compositional borrowing from historical traditions of power portraiture; rich, saturated color; decorative pattern fields; and deliberate tension between figure and ornament. In important works, the background does not stay behind the sitter. Leaves, flowers, or other motifs can overlap the body, collapsing depth and forcing the viewer to negotiate foreground and background. The effect combines grandeur, contemporary identity, and decorative flatness.

The useful transferable principle is **monumental contemporary presence inside an ornamental field that behaves aggressively**. The subject is not merely placed before wallpaper. Pattern competes with, frames, and sometimes invades the figure.

## Visual DNA
- **Scale:** life-size or larger psychological presence, even in a small output.
- **Pose:** borrow the geometry of ceremonial portraiture - upright stance, seated authority, mounted or contrapposto logic, diagonal gesture - but invent the pose.
- **Rendering:** face and hands are the most realistic zones; clothing can be crisp and highly detailed.
- **Background:** flat, saturated ornamental system - botanical, geometric, textile, heraldic, or hybrid - generated specifically for the subject.
- **Overlap:** selected pattern elements cross in front of shoulders, limbs, furniture, or clothing so figure-ground boundaries become unstable.
- **Palette:** jewel tones, saturated greens, deep blues, reds, gold-like yellows, purples; strong color separation.
- **Light:** controlled portrait light with enough modeling to keep the sitter dimensional against the flatter field.
- **Surface contrast:** dimensional human figure versus compressed decorative plane.
- **Psychology:** self-possession, composure, dignity, social presence; avoid empty fashion-model affect.
- **Concept:** contemporary identity is granted the visual scale historically used to communicate power.

## AI preflight
1. What pose geometry communicates authority for this particular subject?
2. What ornamental vocabulary belongs to the subject rather than functioning as generic flowers?
3. Which pattern elements will cross the figure-ground boundary?
4. What part of the face or hands must carry psychological realism?
5. Does the portrait feel monumental without copying an existing ceremonial painting?

## Generation instructions
Create a highly finished contemporary portrait with a strong, invented ceremonial pose. Render the sitter's face, skin, hands, hair, and clothing with disciplined realism. Place the sitter against a dense ornamental field designed from motifs meaningful to the prompt - botanical, architectural, textile, technological, geographic, or symbolic. Keep the ornamental plane flatter than the body. Allow a limited number of motifs to overlap in front of the sitter so the picture oscillates between depth and surface.

Use saturated color intentionally. The sitter must remain visually dominant, but the ornament should be active enough to challenge the hierarchy. If props or furniture appear, treat them as power-bearing elements rather than lifestyle decoration. The result should feel formal, grand, contemporary, and visually lush while remaining an original composition.

## Avoid
- copying a famous painting pose one-to-one
- reproducing recognizable flower arrangements from known portraits
- making the background ordinary wallpaper
- flattening the face to the same degree as the ornament
- costume-drama styling disconnected from the contemporary subject
- decorative luxury with no psychological presence

## Internal quality gate - target 8+
Score: sitter authority; facial realism; invented pose strength; ornamental originality; figure-ground overlap; saturated palette control; dimensional-vs-flat tension; cultural specificity; overall monumentality; originality.

## Research anchors
Smithsonian National Portrait Gallery materials on highly finished likeness, saturated palette, decorative pattern, and the official Barack Obama portrait; museum discussions of contemporary sitters and historical portrait conventions.

# 4. SHADOW ARCHIVE TABLEAU
**Human research reference - DO NOT SEND TO IMAGE MODEL:** Kara Walker  
**Execution mode:** Contemporary independent synthesis. Use the formal logic of cut-paper silhouette and historical contradiction without copying known scenes, characters, or narrative arrangements.

## Research synthesis
MoMA materials describe Walker's black cut-paper silhouettes as an intentionally charged collision between elegant historical craft and disturbing histories of race, violence, sexuality, stereotype, and power. The silhouette matters because it removes interior detail and turns the body into a generalized sign. Walker exploits that reduction rather than treating silhouette as a cute Victorian effect. Positive and negative space become morally unstable: beautiful contour can contain an ugly event; comedy can sit beside horror; apparent innocence can reverse on inspection.

The transferable system is **a deceptively elegant black-and-white historical tableau whose meaning changes through gesture, scale, and contradiction**. The strongest images do not require facial features. Narrative is encoded in contour.

## Visual DNA
- **Medium illusion:** flat black cut-paper silhouettes against white or pale walls/grounds.
- **Edge:** crisp scissors-like contour, occasionally delicate at fingers, fabric, hair, tools, vegetation.
- **Depth:** extremely shallow. Use lateral staging like a frieze, shadow play, or wall installation.
- **Narrative:** multiple figures may interact, but every silhouette must have a readable action.
- **Gesture:** posture carries identity, status, threat, submission, comedy, or reversal.
- **Positive/negative space:** gaps between limbs, props, and bodies are as important as solid black areas.
- **Scale:** shifts in size can create hierarchy, absurdity, memory, or threat.
- **Tone:** elegance and discomfort can coexist. Do not make the image uniformly grim or uniformly whimsical.
- **Setting:** historical cues can appear through costume silhouette, architecture, plants, tools, furniture, or transport, but should not rely on copied scenes.
- **Concept:** stereotype itself can become a visual mechanism to be questioned, not blindly repeated.

## AI preflight
1. What historical or social contradiction is being examined?
2. Can the full action be understood from contour alone?
3. What changes when the viewer notices the second relationship in the scene?
4. Are the silhouettes functioning as meaningful reduction rather than decorative icons?
5. Are any stereotypes present being interrogated rather than reproduced uncritically?

## Generation instructions
Construct an original wall-like tableau using crisp black silhouettes on a pale field. Work laterally. Give each figure an unmistakable action through pose and prop. Build one apparent reading that can be understood immediately, then introduce a second relationship that complicates or reverses it. Use elegant contour and balanced negative space so formal beauty creates tension with the subject matter.

Historical detail should be conveyed through silhouettes - sleeve shape, hat, chair, tool, carriage, plant, architectural profile - rather than rendered texture. If several figures appear, stage them like a theatrical frieze with clear intervals. Keep the surface visually spare. The image should remain legible at thumbnail scale.

## Avoid
- copying known plantation scenes, characters, or exact installations
- turning silhouette into cute clip art
- adding photorealistic interior shading inside black figures
- excessive text that explains the narrative
- random violence without conceptual purpose
- using historical caricature as decoration
- filling every inch; the pale ground is structurally important

## Internal quality gate - target 8+
Score: contour readability; negative-space design; narrative clarity; second-read contradiction; historical cue economy; conceptual intelligence; tonal tension; originality; thumbnail legibility.

## Research anchors
Museum of Modern Art essays, collection texts, and audio commentary on Walker's silhouettes, stereotype as reduction, historical source material, positive/negative space, and the friction between formal elegance and disturbing narrative.

# 5. MONUMENTAL BLACK PRESENCE
**Human research reference - DO NOT SEND TO IMAGE MODEL:** Kerry James Marshall  
**Execution mode:** Contemporary independent synthesis. Use broad formal principles of monumental Black figuration and art-historical scale without reproducing specific figures, scenes, symbols, or compositions.

## Research synthesis
The Museum of Contemporary Art Chicago and related museum sources describe Marshall's practice as a sustained reworking of Western painting genres through unapologetically Black figures, often rendered with extremely dark or near-black pigments. He places Black subjects inside history painting, domestic scenes, landscapes, portraiture, gardens, studios, leisure spaces, and allegorical structures from which they were historically absent. Bright pure colors, flat passages, signs, decorative motifs, speech-like symbols, and art-historical references often coexist with complex spatial staging.

The durable visual principle is not simply 'dark skin.' It is **Black presence treated as the central structural fact of a large, intellectually composed picture**. Skin value, surrounding color, spatial organization, and genre all collaborate.

## Visual DNA
- **Figure:** deep, near-black skin values with deliberate internal modeling; avoid losing facial structure into featureless black.
- **Scale:** figures carry history-painting importance even when engaged in ordinary life.
- **Genre:** domestic life, park, garden, studio, romance, community, leisure, portrait, allegory - ordinary and monumental can coexist.
- **Palette:** saturated primary and secondary colors, bright whites, vivid clothing, colored architecture, dark figure anchors.
- **Spatial logic:** can mix believable space with flattened decorative passages and diagrammatic signs.
- **Details:** cultural objects, books, furniture, signage, hairstyles, clothing, art tools, plants, and symbolic marks may create layered reading.
- **Contrast:** very dark figures against bright, sometimes high-key environments create immediate visual authority.
- **Art history:** borrow genre structures and compositional seriousness, not specific paintings.
- **Narrative:** the subject should be living, doing, making, loving, resting, thinking, or occupying space - not merely representing an identity category.
- **Surface:** painterly but controlled; graphic elements can interrupt illusionistic areas.

## AI preflight
1. What established painting genre is being occupied or revised?
2. What is the subject actually doing?
3. How will very dark skin remain dimensional and expressive?
4. Which environmental details deepen the narrative rather than become clutter?
5. Does the composition confer monumentality without requiring heroic action?

## Generation instructions
Build a large-feeling figurative painting around one or more Black subjects whose presence controls the visual hierarchy. Use deep black or near-black skin values with enough tonal separation to preserve eyes, nose, mouth, cheek structure, hands, and gesture. Surround the figure with clear, saturated colors and carefully selected environmental evidence. Let the setting communicate a full life: domestic, professional, romantic, communal, creative, or leisurely.

Use a composition with the seriousness of history painting but an entirely original scene. Combine dimensional figure painting with occasional flat graphic or decorative elements. Allow objects, signs, plants, patterns, or small symbols to create secondary narratives. The image should reward inspection without requiring explanation.

## Avoid
- treating dark skin as a silhouette unless conceptually required
- generic empowerment-poster poses
- empty luxury staging
- copying known garden, studio, domestic, or barber-shop compositions
- muting the palette until the figure-environment contrast disappears
- reducing the system to 'Black figure plus bright background'

## Internal quality gate - target 8+
Score: Black figure dimensionality; monumental presence; genre intelligence; saturated color control; environmental narrative; art-historical seriousness; ordinary-life specificity; layered secondary detail; originality.

## Research anchors
Museum of Contemporary Art Chicago's *Mastry* materials and related museum texts on monumental Black figuration, pure/dark pigments, historical painting genres, everyday Black life, popular culture, and the rewriting of art-historical visibility.

# 6. CHROMATIC GRISAILLE PORTRAIT
**Human research reference - DO NOT SEND TO IMAGE MODEL:** Amy Sherald  
**Execution mode:** Contemporary independent synthesis. Do not reproduce specific sitters, garments, background colors, props, or known compositions.

## Research synthesis
National Gallery of Art and Whitney materials repeatedly identify a distinctive structural contrast in Sherald's portraits: skin is rendered in grisaille or warm grayscale while clothing, props, and flat backgrounds carry vivid color. The sitter is usually isolated from a detailed setting, creating psychological focus. Direct gaze, controlled pose, careful clothing pattern, and subtle tonal modeling replace conventional realism of skin color. The result is neither monochrome photography nor pop portraiture. It is a deliberate separation of **personhood from expected chromatic coding**.

The transferable system is therefore: **restrained grayscale flesh + saturated chromatic environment + psychologically calm, frontal presence**. The gray must remain luminous and human, not corpse-like or metallic. The flat background must support rather than swallow the sitter.

## Visual DNA
- **Skin:** warm neutral grayscale; broad tonal range but low chroma.
- **Background:** simple, often flat or nearly flat chromatic field; color selected to create clean separation from clothing and flesh.
- **Clothing:** saturated, patterned, graphic, contemporary, and compositionally important.
- **Pose:** frontal or nearly frontal; quiet, balanced, dignified; body language contained rather than theatrical.
- **Gaze:** direct or psychologically self-contained.
- **Light:** soft studio illumination with modest modeling; no hard cinema shadow unless the concept needs it.
- **Edges:** clear figure-ground separation.
- **Props:** few; any prop must add identity or narrative.
- **Space:** shallow; environment is intentionally withheld so the viewer attends to sitter and clothing.
- **Mood:** introspective, composed, specific, unforced.

## AI preflight
1. What emotional state should be communicated through gaze and posture?
2. Which background hue best separates the sitter without becoming decorative noise?
3. Which clothing pattern or silhouette provides chromatic energy?
4. Is the grayscale skin warm, dimensional, and alive?
5. Can any unnecessary prop be removed?

## Generation instructions
Create a portrait with a quiet, centered psychological presence. Render the sitter's skin entirely in a nuanced warm grayscale with careful facial modeling and natural features. Keep hair, eyes, lips, and hands integrated into the grayscale logic unless a deliberate small accent is conceptually necessary. Use vivid clothing or a patterned garment as the primary chromatic structure. Place the sitter against a simple flat or minimally modulated color field selected to complement the garment rather than match it.

Keep the pose restrained. Avoid fashion-model exaggeration. Let the sitter look at the viewer or slightly away with a self-contained expression. Use soft, even light. The image should feel painted and intentional, not like a photo with a grayscale filter applied to skin.

## Avoid
- gray skin that reads as metallic, dead, or desaturated by accident
- busy environmental backgrounds
- generic beauty photography
- copying a known garment, color pairing, or pose
- neon saturation on every element
- over-smoothed skin or excessive glamour retouching
- dramatic action poses

## Internal quality gate - target 8+
Score: grayscale flesh dimensionality; sitter psychology; garment color/pattern strength; background color intelligence; pose restraint; facial specificity; painterly coherence; originality; simplicity. Any score below 8 requires revision.

## Research anchors
National Gallery of Art and Whitney Museum of American Art materials on Sherald's grisaille skin, vivid clothing, flat backgrounds, direct gaze, and portrait psychology.

# 7. RHINESTONE RETRO SOVEREIGNTY
**Human research reference - DO NOT SEND TO IMAGE MODEL:** Mickalene Thomas  
**Execution mode:** Contemporary independent synthesis. Use broad principles of embellished portraiture, collage, pattern, and retro domestic staging without reproducing known sitters, furniture layouts, poses, or signature compositions.

## Research synthesis
Brooklyn Museum materials describe Thomas's portraits as celebrations of Black women built through painting, photography, collage, rhinestones, acrylic, enamel, pattern, and references to art history and 1970s Black popular culture. Interiors matter. Upholstery, wood paneling, textiles, wallpaper, mirrors, plants, rugs, and clothing form dense patterned environments. Rhinestones and reflective materials do more than add glamour: they turn the surface into an object that changes with light. Many compositions negotiate beauty, self-fashioning, sexuality, confidence, domestic space, and the history of who has been granted the right to occupy the reclining or monumental portrait.

The transferable principle is **a sovereign sitter surrounded by competing layers of pattern, reflective surface, retro interior design, and collage logic**. The image must feel assembled as much as painted.

## Visual DNA
- **Subject:** confident Black woman or women presented as self-possessed rather than observed.
- **Pose:** seated, reclining, frontal, or diagonally arranged; relaxed authority is more important than rigid formality.
- **Interior:** richly designed domestic or lounge environment with period-informed textiles and furniture.
- **Pattern:** multiple patterns can coexist - clothing, upholstery, wall treatment, rug, curtain - but need visual hierarchy.
- **Material:** rhinestone-like reflective points, enamel gloss, acrylic paint, cut-paper or photographic collage zones.
- **Palette:** warm browns, oranges, avocado greens, magenta, red, gold, cream, black, jewel tones; high chromatic density.
- **Surface:** alternate matte painted passages with sparkling/high-gloss accents.
- **Composition:** figure integrated into decor without disappearing; face remains psychological anchor.
- **Reference logic:** art-historical portrait/reclining traditions can inform geometry but not be copied literally.
- **Mood:** glamorous, intimate, assertive, sensual without reducing the sitter to an object.

## AI preflight
1. What gives the sitter visual sovereignty?
2. Which three pattern families can coexist without collapsing into noise?
3. Where will reflective embellishment concentrate to guide the eye?
4. What period cues are authentic rather than costume-like?
5. Does the face remain the psychological center despite the dense surface?

## Generation instructions
Build an embellished portrait around a confident sitter in a richly patterned interior. Use a deliberate retro design vocabulary: tactile upholstery, geometric textiles, wood, patterned wall surfaces, plants, mirrors, shag or woven rugs, sculptural furniture. Introduce collage logic by allowing some areas to feel cut, layered, or photographically inserted rather than uniformly painted. Place reflective bead or rhinestone-like highlights selectively along clothing, furniture edges, jewelry, or decorative zones so light creates a second surface rhythm.

Control density through zones: one highly patterned zone, one medium-density zone, and one quieter zone. Maintain realistic facial structure while permitting clothing and environment to become more graphic. The sitter should look as though the room extends their identity rather than merely surrounds them.

## Avoid
- random glitter across the entire frame
- generic disco styling without domestic specificity
- reducing the image to a 1970s costume shoot
- copying known sofas, poses, or portrait arrangements
- making every surface equally busy
- sexualized posing without subject agency
- photorealism with no collage/material tension

# 22. DIGNIFIED SOCIAL CINEMA

**Execution mode:** Historical-reference synthesis. Use humanist documentary, cinematic framing, social context, fashion sensitivity, and narrative sequencing without recreating a known photograph, location, subject, or pose.

## Research synthesis
Gordon Parks Foundation and museum materials show that Parks moved fluently among documentary photography, portraiture, fashion, photo essays, film, literature, and social reporting. His strongest photographic language combines formal elegance with direct social observation. He photographed segregation, poverty, crime, labor, family, artists, celebrities, fashion, and everyday Black life without reducing people to conditions. His fashion work adds another layer. Clothing can move through real streets rather than stay inside studio space; architectural lines and urban backgrounds give a fashion figure cinematic scale. His portraits frequently use controlled directional light, shadow, mirrors, windows, framing devices, or work environments. Photo essays establish narrative through different shot distances: establishing view, environmental portrait, action, detail, intimate reaction, and closing image.

The transferable system is therefore **cinematic humanist photography in which light, environment, and sequence reveal social reality without stealing dignity from the subject**.

## Core modes
### Mode A - Social documentary
Real environment, observed action, contextual evidence, ethical distance, strong human center.

### Mode B - Environmental portrait
Subject posed or paused inside the place that explains their work, life, pressure, identity, or ambition.

### Mode C - Street fashion cinema
Elegant clothing and controlled body line moving through real architecture, traffic, streets, stairs, or public space.

### Mode D - Narrative photo essay
A coherent 5-10 image sequence that changes distance and emotional function rather than repeating the same portrait.

## Visual DNA
- **Framing:** deliberate and cinematic; doorways, mirrors, railings, windows, counters, stair rails, furniture, and street geometry can frame or divide the subject.
- **Camera distance:** medium-wide environmental views are essential; close-ups are earned by narrative context.
- **Light:** natural window light, practical interior light, directional side light, hard daylight, or controlled flash. Shadow can carry emotional weight.
- **Black and white:** rich blacks, luminous midtones, readable highlights, strong local contrast; avoid crushed detail everywhere.
- **Color:** when used, keep documentary realism. Color should emerge from clothing and environment rather than modern teal-orange grading.
- **Subject:** psychologically present, never treated as scenery. Direct gaze can create accountability; off-camera gaze can reveal interiority.
- **Environment:** signs, furniture, uniforms, architecture, work tools, street conditions, family objects, and public infrastructure provide evidence.
- **Fashion:** emphasize silhouette, gesture, fabric movement, and relationship to architecture.
- **Moment:** favor consequential pauses - before action, after action, waiting, thinking, watching, moving.
- **Social meaning:** built from observable conditions, not added slogans.

## Composition architecture
1. Identify the human subject.
2. Identify the environmental fact that changes how the subject is understood.
3. Find a framing structure that visually binds subject and fact.
4. Choose a light direction that reveals rather than glamorizes.
5. Wait for or construct a gesture that makes the picture emotionally specific.

Use foreground obstruction sparingly: a doorway edge, window reflection, railing, counter, or partial silhouette can make the viewer feel physically located. Do not hide the subject merely to look cinematic.

## Photo-essay sequencing protocol
For a series, do not generate ten variations of the same frame. Build:
1. **Establishing frame** - place and condition.
2. **Environmental portrait** - person within that condition.
3. **Action frame** - work, movement, interaction.
4. **Detail** - hands, object, sign, clothing, tool, evidence.
5. **Relationship frame** - person with family, colleague, institution, street, or audience.
6. **Interior reaction** - quiet face or gesture.
7. **Contradiction frame** - visual evidence that complicates the obvious story.
8. **Closing image** - unresolved but emotionally conclusive.

## AI preflight
1. What observable social or personal reality does the environment reveal?
2. Is the subject still presented with agency and individuality?
3. What framing device makes the context visually inseparable from the person?
4. What type of light belongs naturally to this location?
5. Is the decisive emotional information carried by gesture, gaze, relationship, or environment?
6. If fashion is involved, is fabric interacting with real space rather than floating in a studio fantasy?
7. If generating a series, does each frame perform a different narrative job?

## Generation instructions
Create a photograph with documentary credibility and cinematic discipline. Place the subject in a real, information-rich environment. Keep enough depth of field for key contextual evidence to remain legible. Use directional natural or practical light, carefully balancing deep shadow with readable facial information. Frame through architecture when useful. Capture a gesture or pause that feels psychologically unforced.

For black-and-white output, prioritize tonal separation: deep blacks, textured shadow, luminous skin highlights, moderate grain, and detailed midtones. For color, preserve realistic period and location color. Do not impose contemporary commercial grading.

When photographing fashion, let the person move. Use streets, stairs, stone, storefront glass, statuary, railings, wind, and changing light to create counterpoint with the garment. The figure should remain elegant but physically located in the world.

## Avoid
- poverty as visual spectacle
- generic 'vintage documentary' filters
- extreme film grain hiding weak composition
- shallow depth of field that erases social context
- glamorous lighting that contradicts the setting
- copying famous Parks images or poses
- treating every subject as solemn; humor, style, intimacy, and aspiration are also part of life
- overdirecting expressions

## Internal quality gate - target 8+

## Two operating modes

Dense faceting, limited ochre/gray/brown palette, shallow depth, interpenetrating figure and ground, repeated edge directions.

Larger simplified shapes, stronger color, paper/textile/newspaper-like fragments, partial lettering, explicit collage, iconic object clues.

3. Are figure and background using the same planar grammar?
4. Is this analytical or synthetic mode?
5. Has the subject been reconstructed, or merely faceted cosmetically?

## Generation instructions

## AI preflight

2. What physical distance exists between people, windows, doors, or street?
3. What geometric light shape controls the composition?
4. What can be removed to increase stillness?

## Generation instructions
Choose an ordinary location and simplify it into a few strong architectural masses. ## Avoid

- melodramatic crying or confrontation
- busy crowds

## Research synthesis

## Visual DNA

2. What three large zones represent stages or consequences?
3. What 8-15 mini-scenes support that theme?
4. What rules govern the invented creature ecology?
5. Which three anchor forms help the viewer navigate the density?

- random monster collage

- one giant creature dominating every narrative

# 45. ANGULAR NERVOUS FIGURATION
**Human research reference - DO NOT SEND TO IMAGE MODEL:** Egon Schiele  
**Execution mode:** Historical-reference synthesis. Use taut contour, exposed paper, angular anatomy, compressed color, and psychological pose without copying a known sitter, self-portrait, hand position, costume, or drawing.

## Research synthesis
Museum materials describe Schiele as a virtuoso draftsman whose figures often appear angular, contorted, elongated, exposed, and psychologically raw. Line carries enormous pressure. The body may be isolated on bare or lightly washed paper, leaving large negative areas. Hands, joints, shoulders, knees, neck, and facial planes can become sharp expressive structures. Color is often selective - ochre, green, rust, red, black, pale flesh - applied in patches rather than fully modelled academic volume.

The transferable principle is **psychology transmitted through contour tension and pose before color or setting enters**.

## Visual DNA
- **Line:** jagged, nervous, variable, dry, decisive; contour can double or break.
- **Body:** elongated limbs, sharp joints, compressed torso, awkward weight, expressive hands.
- **Pose:** self-conscious, folded, twisted, leaning, seated, crouched, or sharply turned.
- **Ground:** large areas of cream/off-white paper left untouched.
- **Color:** selective washes and patches; pale skin with ochre, rust, green, red, gray, black accents.
- **Shading:** limited; line and localized wash create form.
- **Face:** intense eyes, angular cheeks, compressed mouth, sparse detail.
- **Clothing:** if present, can become dark graphic shape around exposed skin.
- **Composition:** figure often floats or barely contacts the ground.
- **Mood:** exposed, anxious, confrontational, intimate, brittle.

## AI preflight
1. Can the pose communicate the emotion as pure contour?
2. Which joints or hands carry the strongest tension?
3. How much paper can remain untouched?
4. Where are the 3-5 necessary color patches?
5. Is anatomical distortion expressive rather than accidental?

## Generation instructions
Draw the figure first with a tense, variable contour. Exaggerate only those anatomical relationships that strengthen the pose: neck length, shoulder angle, wrist bend, knee projection, finger spread. Leave most of the paper visible. Add sparse watercolor/gouache-like patches to cheeks, joints, hair, clothing, and selected skin zones. Do not fully fill the body with smooth color.

Let the line wobble, break, thicken, or double where psychological pressure increases. Keep the surrounding environment almost absent unless one chair, garment, or floor line is essential.

## Avoid
- copying known self-portrait poses
- academic smooth anatomy
- full scenic backgrounds
- fashion illustration prettiness
- airbrushed skin
- distortion applied evenly to every limb
- decorative scribble unrelated to contour

## Internal quality gate - target 8+
Score: contour tension; pose psychology; expressive anatomy; hand/joint emphasis; negative paper; selective color; line variation; raw immediacy; originality.

## Research anchors
Museum materials on Schiele's virtuosic line, penetrating portraiture, angular and contorted bodies, expressive distortion, and psychologically exposed figuration.

# 46. DISTORTED EXISTENTIAL CHAMBER
**Human research reference - DO NOT SEND TO IMAGE MODEL:** Francis Bacon  
**Execution mode:** Historical-reference synthesis. Use figurative distortion, isolated bodies, geometric/stage-like enclosures, serial panels, and visceral paint behavior without copying a known pope, portrait, crucifixion, triptych, cage, or pose.

## Research synthesis
MoMA materials describe Bacon's postwar figurative painting as powerful and troubling, with abstracted humans and animals repeatedly placed in intentionally disorienting geometric spaces. Triptych formats let related images sit side by side like film frames without resolving into continuous narrative. Portrait heads can appear to turn, smear, compress, or reorganize across panels. Dark voids, flat color fields, cage-like lines, curved platforms, door-like niches, and sparse staged rooms intensify isolation.

The transferable principle is **recognizable flesh under perceptual and psychological pressure**. Distortion should feel like motion, compression, impact, memory, or unstable seeing - not random liquify effects.

## Visual DNA
- **Figure:** recognizable human or animal anatomy partially smeared, twisted, compressed, stretched, or reassembled.
- **Head:** face can contain directional drag, duplicated features, or several temporal positions at once.
- **Space:** sparse geometric enclosure - box, rail, circular platform, stage, niche, room corner, flat field.
- **Background:** large uninterrupted zones of black, orange, red, pink, gray, beige, green, or deep blue can intensify the isolated body.
- **Paint:** scraped, wiped, dragged, scumbled, thickened, and abruptly thin within the same figure.
- **Triptych/series:** related views can shift body, time, or psychological state from panel to panel.
- **Crop:** close head studies or isolated full bodies; surrounding emptiness matters.
- **Light:** often less naturalistic than Caravaggio; figure may appear lit as an object on a stage.
- **Geometry vs flesh:** rigid lines create tension with unstable body.
- **Mood:** exposed, anxious, visceral, lonely, compressed, unstable.

## Distortion rule
Every distortion must have a directional cause:
- lateral drag -> turning head / motion / temporal overlap
- vertical compression -> weight / collapse
- radial smear -> impact / scream / pressure
- duplicated edge -> motion / uncertainty
- erased feature -> memory loss / perceptual instability

Do not distort every region equally.

## AI preflight
1. What psychological or temporal pressure is deforming the figure?
2. Which 20-40 percent of anatomy remains stable enough for recognition?
3. What geometric enclosure opposes the flesh?
4. What large color field isolates the subject?
5. Would a single panel or a serial/triptych structure communicate the idea better?

## Generation instructions
Place one original figure or portrait inside a sparse geometric chamber. Establish the stable anatomy first. Distort selected zones through directional paint behavior: drag part of a face sideways, compress a shoulder, duplicate a jaw edge, smear a moving hand, or erase a portion of torso. Keep other parts sharply recognizable so the distortion has force.

Use one broad background color or dark void with minimal scenery. Add a thin geometric enclosure, platform, rail, frame, or stage line that remains cleaner than the body. If using multiple panels, let each panel show a related but non-identical perceptual state. Paint should alternate between raw smear and controlled edge.

## Avoid
- generic horror gore
- Photoshop liquify across the entire face
- copying famous pope/scream imagery
- cinematic dungeon backgrounds
- excessive props
- distortion with no directional logic
- smooth 3D flesh with distortion only in geometry

## Internal quality gate - target 8+
Score: figure recognizability; purposeful distortion; flesh/geometry tension; paint physicality; spatial isolation; color-field strength; serial logic when relevant; psychological pressure; originality.

## Research anchors
MoMA and Metropolitan Museum materials on Bacon's troubling postwar figures, abstracted bodies in disorienting geometric spaces, repeated series and triptychs, black voids, closely cropped heads, and temporal/panning-like shifts across panels.

# 47. GESTURAL FIGURE-FIELD COLLISION
**Human research reference - DO NOT SEND TO IMAGE MODEL:** Willem de Kooning  
**Execution mode:** Historical-reference synthesis. Use vigorous revision, figure/abstraction instability, gestural brushwork, scraping, and color collision without reproducing a known Woman-series pose, face, mouth, collage fragment, or composition.

## Research synthesis
Metropolitan Museum and MoMA materials emphasize de Kooning's refusal to choose permanently between figuration and abstraction. Figures can appear to assemble and disintegrate inside active fields of paint. Vigorous strokes register speed, agitation, humor, revision, and tension. Charcoal drawing, paint, scraping, wiping, drips, collage, and reworked contours can coexist. In the Women paintings, body parts sometimes remain strongly recognizable while other regions dissolve into surrounding marks.

The transferable principle is **the figure and the painting field fighting for the same territory**. A successful image never looks like a finished portrait with abstract brushstrokes pasted behind it.

## Visual DNA
- **Brushwork:** sweeping, slashing, looping, broad, wet-on-wet, dry scrape, rapid correction.
- **Figure:** partially legible; face, eyes, mouth, limbs, torso clues can appear and disappear.
- **Field:** active enough to merge with figure; similar colors and marks cross body/background boundary.
- **Revision:** redraw, scrape, repaint, shift contours, leave prior positions visible.
- **Color:** flesh pink, white, black, cream, orange, red, green, blue, yellow; combinations can be abrasive and bright.
- **Drawing:** charcoal/pencil-like lines may sit over or under paint.
- **Edge:** unstable - some contours sharp, others erased or swallowed.
- **Space:** shallow and turbulent rather than deep illusionistic room.
- **Energy:** large body-arm gestures visible in mark scale.
- **Mood:** tense, humorous, aggressive, sensual, unstable.

## AI preflight
1. Which anatomical clues must remain visible?
2. Which parts of the body will dissolve into the field?
3. Where is a visible revision or abandoned contour needed?
4. What color collision creates the dominant tension?
5. Do background and body share the same mark language?

## Generation instructions
Establish a loose figure with charcoal-like drawing. Paint over and through it with broad gestures. Reassert only selected body clues after the first abstraction pass. Scrape or wipe one area and redraw it in a shifted position so process remains visible. Let at least three major strokes cross from background into body without respecting contour.

Use color in blocks, swipes, and broken passages rather than clean local filling. Keep the image shallow. If a facial feature becomes highly defined, allow neighboring anatomy to remain unstable so the figure feels continuously assembled before the viewer.

## Avoid
- a clean portrait on an abstract-expressionist background
- copying recognizable Woman-series faces
- uniform brush size
- digital smear filters
- perfectly balanced color harmony
- hiding all revisions
- total abstraction with no figure clues when figure mode is intended

## Internal quality gate - target 8+
Score: figure-field fusion; gestural force; revision visibility; anatomical clue balance; edge instability; color collision; paint/drawing interplay; process energy; originality.

## Research anchors
Metropolitan Museum and MoMA materials on de Kooning's simultaneous figuration/abstraction, vigorous gestural brushwork, bodies forming and falling apart, visible speed/agitation/tension, collage, and loose marks dissolving figure into field.

# 48. LUMINOUS EMOTIONAL FIELD
**Human research reference - DO NOT SEND TO IMAGE MODEL:** Mark Rothko  
**Execution mode:** Historical-reference synthesis focused on the mature Color Field format. Do not reproduce a known palette, rectangle proportion, chapel grouping, or painting.

## Research synthesis
National Gallery of Art and MoMA materials describe Rothko's mature format as two or three large, soft-edged zones of color stacked vertically against a colored ground. The apparent simplicity hides multiple thin layers, varying transparency, stains, undercolors, feathery transitions, and subtle surface differences. Edges are softened rather than mechanically rectangular. Underlayers show through and make fields seem to glow, hover, recede, or press forward. Rothko repeatedly insisted that the purpose was not formal color harmony alone but direct emotional experience.

The transferable principle is **large-scale color relationships behaving like presences rather than graphic rectangles**.

## Visual DNA
- **Format:** vertical or near-vertical large field; usually 2-4 major hovering zones.
- **Shape:** approximate rectangle, softened, rounded, irregular, feathered, sometimes cut off by canvas edge.
- **Ground:** active color, not neutral backing.
- **Layer:** many thin stains/glazes with undercolor visible through top color.
- **Edge:** transitional zone is critical; colors mingle, vibrate, or expose prior layers.
- **Surface:** matte to softly luminous; avoid glossy graphic finish.
- **Color:** any palette can work if emotionally coherent; relationships matter more than fashionable color matching.
- **Scale:** immersive; color areas should feel bodily in proportion.
- **Space:** ambiguous hovering - forms can advance/recede without perspective.
- **Mood:** tragedy, ecstasy, dread, warmth, silence, radiance, gravity; choose one or two dominant states.

## AI preflight
1. What emotional state is being pursued?
2. What ground color establishes that state before rectangles are added?
3. What undercolor will peek through each main field?
4. Are the edges soft and alive rather than blurred uniformly?
5. Do the proportions feel immersive rather than logo-like?

## Generation instructions
Choose an emotional objective first. Lay down a colored ground in several thin, uneven washes so subtle value variation remains. Add two or three large hovering color fields. Each should contain at least one underlayer whose color appears through thin upper paint, especially near the edge. Soften boundaries with feathery brush or rag-like transitions while preserving an irregular shape.

Avoid hard graphic corners. Let one field feel heavier and one more luminous. Keep the surface quiet enough that small hue changes matter. The final image should reward sustained viewing rather than deliver an immediate symbol.

## Avoid
- hard-edged modernist rectangles
- flat digital fills
- copying famous red/orange/black arrangements
- decorative gradient backgrounds
- tiny poster-scale proportions
- equal spacing and perfect geometry
- treating the system as simple color matching

## Internal quality gate - target 8+
Score: ground activity; layered transparency; edge vibration; color-field presence; emotional coherence; hovering spatial effect; proportion/scale; matte-luminous surface; originality.

## Research anchors
National Gallery of Art and MoMA materials on soft-edged vertically stacked rectangles, thin layered stains, undercolors, hazy matte surfaces, blurred corners, floating fields, perceptual depth, and Rothko's emphasis on basic human emotions over color relationships alone.

# 50. CONCEPTUAL CRAFT DISRUPTION
**Human research reference - DO NOT SEND TO IMAGE MODEL:** Ai Weiwei  
**Execution mode:** Contemporary independent synthesis. Use broad principles of traditional craft, ordinary/found material, repetition, minimal form, cultural recontextualization, and conceptual scale without reproducing famous sunflower seeds, bicycles, stools, vases, LEGO portraits, surveillance works, or specific installations.

## Research synthesis
Smithsonian Hirshhorn materials describe Ai's broad practice across sculpture, photography, audio, video, architecture, internet media, and site-specific installation. The transferable principle is **take a material with an existing cultural or social history, change its context or scale with extreme conceptual clarity, and let the material's prior meaning collide with its new role**.

## Visual DNA
- **Material:** traditional craft material, architectural salvage, found daily object, modular manufactured unit, wood joinery, porcelain-like ceramic, brick, metal, textile, stone, glass, digital/pixel module.
- **Form:** simple, often Minimal or grid-like; conceptual complexity should not require visual clutter.
- **Repetition:** hundreds or thousands of units can turn individual objects into a field.
- **Craft:** traditional joinery, carving, firing, weaving, casting, or hand assembly can be visibly precise.
- **Recontextualization:** ordinary or historical object moved into an unexpected scale, arrangement, function, or display system.
- **Individual/collective:** one unit remains identifiable while mass quantity creates a second reading.
- **Evidence:** labels, breakage, repair, provenance marks, grain, glaze, joinery, wear can carry meaning.
- **Space:** installation often responds to floor, wall, atrium, public plaza, architecture, or circulation path.
- **Media:** internet/surveillance/pixel logic can be translated into modular visual structure when relevant.
- **Concept:** value, authenticity, preservation, destruction, memory, institutions, individual/collective relationship, public record.

## AI preflight
1. What material already has a history before the artwork begins?
2. What single transformation changes its meaning most efficiently?
3. Is the piece based on one object, a modular field, or an architectural installation?
4. If using repetition, can each individual unit still be recognized?
5. What will the viewer understand physically before reading any wall text?
6. Is the composition original and independent of famous Ai installations?

## Generation instructions
Choose one culturally or materially specific object category relevant to the user's subject. Research or define its construction accurately. Recontextualize it through one dominant operation: repeat it at massive scale, disassemble and rejoin it, suspend it, flatten it into a grid, reconstruct it in another material, arrange fragments into a precise field, or turn its manufacturing logic into architecture.

Preserve physical evidence - joinery, grain, glaze, chips, labels, seams, repairs, or module boundaries. If using mass repetition, show both wide-scale pattern and close-range individual units.

- false claims about historical provenance
- aesthetic destruction with no conceptual reason

Score: material-history specificity; transformation clarity; craft credibility; simple-form strength; individual/collective tension; installation-scale logic; evidence/provenance detail; conceptual legibility; originality.

# FINAL AGENT EXECUTION PROTOCOL

## Step 1 - Select one branded system
Use exactly one primary system unless the user explicitly requests a hybrid. Do not insert the research-reference artist name into the generation prompt.

## Step 2 - Extract the non-negotiables
For the selected system, copy into working memory:
- core visual mechanism
- composition logic
- palette logic
- material/mark behavior
- spatial logic
- conceptual engine
- avoid list
- quality-gate dimensions

## Step 3 - Translate the user's subject
Do not simply apply a surface filter. Ask how the user's subject behaves *inside the selected visual system*.

Examples:
- In Dignified Social Cinema, ask what environment reveals about the person.
- In Cartographic Velocity Field, turn subject/context into structural, atmospheric, and gestural layers.
- In Story-Quilt Chronicle, convert subject into narrator + central action + border memory.
- In Urban Paradox Stencil, build subject + symbol + contradiction + environment.
- In Luminous Emotional Field, convert the requested emotional objective into color relationships, not imagery.

## Step 4 - Draft before rendering
Generate a private concept specification containing:
1. composition
2. palette
3. light/value
4. material behavior
5. subject treatment
6. spatial behavior
7. conceptual meaning
8. originality check

## Step 5 - Run the quality gate
Score every named quality category in the selected system from 1-10. Any category under 8 requires redesign before generation. Do not average a weak category away.

## Step 6 - Run the originality gate
Ask:
- Does this resemble a specific known artwork?
- Is the composition too close to a famous image?
- Am I using a signature character/motif as a shortcut?
- For contemporary-reference systems, is this an independent work based on broad formal principles?

If any answer creates concern, change the composition, motif, prop, palette, and/or narrative architecture before rendering.

## Step 7 - Render
Only after the concept and originality gates pass.

## Step 8 - Post-render critique
Rate the actual output against the same quality dimensions. If any important category scores below 8, identify the exact cause and regenerate with a targeted correction. Do not rewrite the entire system unless repeated failures reveal a systemic weakness.
