# Pre-production 05: Steal the grammar

When the operator names a reference video they admire, study it the way a musician studies a record: take the grammar, never the content.

## The method

1. Get the reference video as a local file. Extract one frame every half second with FFmpeg into a `refs/frames/` folder and study them in order.
2. Write down the GRAMMAR only: palette behavior (how color moves, not which colors), type behavior (how words enter and exit, not the words), shot lengths, transition types, camera moves, texture and grain, how the rhythm builds.
3. Write down what you will NOT take: its footage, its logos, its characters, its copy, its music. Name these explicitly so the line stays visible.
4. Translate the grammar onto the brand bible: same rhythm, brand's colors, brand's type, brand's assets.

## The rule

Take the grammar of the reference, never its content, logos, or characters. If a frame of your video could be mistaken for a frame of the reference, you took too much. The critic's brand-accuracy criterion checks this.
