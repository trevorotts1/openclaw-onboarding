# Pre-production 06: The director's brief

The director's brief is the single document the animation author, the voiceover writer, and the critic all work from. Write it from `references/directors-brief-template.md`, then get the operator's explicit approval before any animation code is written. A change after approval restarts the brief; it does not get smuggled into a scene file.

## What makes a brief good

- The one-liner from chapter 04 sits at the top and every scene serves it.
- Each scene has a job stated as a verb: establish, prove, contrast, resolve. A scene without a verb gets cut or merged.
- The voiceover script is final text, not placeholders. Placeholders in the brief become placeholders in the video.
- The beat plan says where the score breathes and where it pushes, so the synthesized score can be shaped to the story (tempo, energy per scene, where the hits land).
- The shot list names the transition into and out of every scene. No scene is allowed to "just cut" without the cut being a deliberate choice on a bar line.

## Approval

The operator reads the brief and approves it in writing (a yes in chat counts). Until then, no scene files, no voiceover requests, no renders. This is the cheapest place in the whole pipeline to fix a bad idea.
