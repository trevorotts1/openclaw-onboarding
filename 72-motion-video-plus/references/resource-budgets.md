# Resource Budgets: Measured Capacity and Shared Render Reservations

Directive MG-028. Code: `runtime/src/resources/` (types, profiles,
preflight, budget). Tests: `runtime/tests/run-resources.ts`.
Measurement driver: `runtime/tests/measure-resources.ts`. Evidence:
`tests/evidence/mg028/measurements.json`, regenerated by the
measurement case inside `run-resources.ts` on every run.

## Why a budget

Render concurrency used to be a per-caller choice. Ten authors with
eight render workers each can overload a client machine even when
every individual count looks reasonable, because nothing accounted
for the sum. The budget is the single accounting point: before any
browser or encoder launches, the operation reserves capacity against
a machine-wide budget, and the reservation is what gets counted.

## The model

- A **reservation** declares one operation's footprint: estimated
  peak memory, browser units, encoder units, intermediate disk, and
  the engine's nested concurrency (see Profiles).
- Reservations live in an **on-disk ledger**: one JSON record per
  reservation in `<shared runtime state>/render-budget/reservations/`.
  The ledger is shared by every render process on the machine, so
  two agent processes rendering at once account into the same
  totals. Check-and-create runs inside a short mkdir mutex, so two
  processes cannot both pass the capacity check and overbook.
- Holders **heartbeat** their record. A record whose owner process
  is gone is reaped by the next acquire or snapshot; a record whose
  heartbeat has been silent for the hard-stale bound (the larger of
  five times the 120 s stale threshold or 10 minutes) is reaped even
  if its pid was recycled. A crashed render therefore frees its
  capacity with no operator action.
- Within one process, holds are **reference-counted per job**:
  adapter operations nest (a Remotion final is a video render plus a
  poster still) and must neither deadlock against themselves nor
  double-count.
- An acquire that cannot fit **waits**, polling every 100 ms, honors
  cancellation promptly, and fails after the acquire timeout (20
  minutes by default) naming the current load. The failure is raised
  before anything launches: no browser, no encoder, no partial work.

This is a cooperative accounting budget, not an OS sandbox: it
bounds every render that goes through the production adapters,
which is the render path this skill owns. It is not a cgroup; a
scene far outside the measured fixture family can exceed its
profile's estimate, and the margin plus the host reserve are the
mitigations, stated plainly rather than hidden.

Two limits on the word "shared" in the ledger description above,
stated just as plainly. First, shared means shared within one
checkout: state-directory discovery walks up from the runtime to
the enclosing checkout's `shared-utils/motion-render-runtime`
state, so two checkouts of this repo on one physical machine keep
two separate ledgers, and renders in one checkout never account
against renders in the other. Second, when no enclosing checkout
state is found, the budget falls back to a per-user temp directory
that nothing else shares; the policy records which case applied
(`sharedStateDir`), and a render on the temp fallback accounts
only against itself. Operators who run several checkouts on one
box should point `MOTION_RENDER_BUDGET_DIR` at a single directory
so the accounting is genuinely machine-wide.

## Measured capacity

Measured on the build machine (2 cores, 7935 MB RAM, Linux x64,
runtime lock-314fc289b233ca9e, Remotion 4.0.535, Hyperframes
0.8.155) by rendering the shipped C05 fixture job (180 frames,
1920x1080, 30 fps) through each production adapter in a child
process while a parent sampled the child's whole process tree via
/proc every 50 ms. Per-phase peaks (maxima across the runs):

| Operation | Peak tree RSS | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Remotion preview render | 1283 MB | 1 browser main; render phases 1264-1283 MB across runs |
| Remotion render sequence, cold bundle | 1469 MB | whole-sequence peak including the one-time bundle build |
| Remotion still, warm bundle | 634 MB | browser startup plus one frame dominates |
| Hyperframes render | 985 MB | peaked at **4 Chromium main processes** inside the one operation |
| Hyperframes validate | 895 MB | compositions + lint + check |
| Hyperframes snapshot (still) | 812 MB | CLI + browser startup + capture |
| Standalone ffmpeg encode (libx264 crf 16) | 517 MB | the encode share of a render, isolated |

Wall times from the same runs are recorded in the evidence but are
**not** capacity numbers: this box is shared with other build work,
so wall times inflate under contention. RSS peaks were stable
across runs (within about 10 percent).

Two findings worth naming:

1. **Nested concurrency is real and asymmetric.** Remotion runs one
   browser whose tabs the adapter controls. The Hyperframes CLI,
   even at one worker, peaked at four Chromium main processes
   inside a single render. Counting "browsers" per operation would
   understate Hyperframes by 4x; the profiles are therefore sized
   from whole-tree peaks, and the budget counts operations.
2. **Browser startup is a first-class cost.** A warm Remotion still
   (634 MB, seconds of wall time for one frame) and a Hyperframes
   snapshot (812 MB) show that launching a browser to touch one
   frame costs a large fraction of a full render's footprint.
   Batching frames per launch matters more than shaving frames.

### Profiles

Estimates are measured peak x 1.30, rounded up to 16 MB. The margin
covers sampling granularity and scene variation; it is a stated
constant (`MEASUREMENT_MARGIN` in `src/resources/profiles.ts`), not
a hidden fudge.

| Profile | Estimate | Browser | Encoder | Disk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| remotion renderFinal / renderPreview | 1920 MB | 1 | 1 | 512 MB |
| remotion renderStill / inspectMotion | 832 MB | 1 | 0 | 64 MB |
| hyperframes renderFinal / renderPreview | 1296 MB | 1 | 1 | 512 MB |
| hyperframes renderStill / inspectMotion | 1056 MB | 1 | 0 | 64 MB |
| hyperframes validate | 1168 MB | 1 | 0 | 128 MB |
| hyperframes encode (ffmpeg step) | 688 MB | 0 | 1 | 256 MB |

One caveat the measurements force into the open: a Remotion still
as the **very first** operation against a fresh runtime also pays
the one-time bundle build and can transiently approach the render
peak (1469 MB observed). The host reserve absorbs that one-off;
workflows that render stills first on a fresh runtime should expect
it.

## Preflight and the host reserve

`preflightReport()` (src/resources/preflight.ts) measures the
machine and derives the policy; this is the TypeScript preflight
the MG-028 brief allows in place of a Python twin, chosen because
the scheduler and both consumers live in this package: the policy
math exists exactly once, in the language that enforces it.

Derivation on the build machine:

- Total memory 7935 MB.
- **Host reserve 1587 MB** (the larger of 1024 MB or 20 percent of
  total). Rationale: the OS, the agent processes, the dispatcher,
  and the owner's active services live in the reserve. A render
  storm must not starve them: a machine that swaps or OOM-kills the
  agent produces failed renders and a broken session, not just slow
  renders.
- Memory budget 6348 MB = total minus reserve.
- Browser cap **2**: how many of the heaviest measured profile fit
  the memory budget (4), bounded by the core count (2). Browsers are
  CPU-bound long before a big machine runs out of RAM.
- Encoder cap **2** (one per core). Disk ceiling 20000 MB of
  declared intermediate storage.

Operator overrides (environment): `MOTION_RENDER_BUDGET_DIR`,
`MOTION_RENDER_HOST_RESERVE_MB`, `MOTION_RENDER_MEMORY_BUDGET_MB`,
`MOTION_RENDER_MAX_BROWSER_UNITS`, `MOTION_RENDER_MAX_ENCODE_UNITS`,
`MOTION_RENDER_MAX_DISK_MB`, `MOTION_RENDER_ACQUIRE_TIMEOUT_MS`.
Budgets are raised or lowered explicitly through these, never by
editing measured profiles.

## How the adapters use it

**Remotion** acquires at operation granularity: `renderStill`,
`renderPreview`, and `renderFinal` each hold one reservation from
before composition selection (which is what launches the browser)
to the verified end of the render, released in a `finally`. The
reservation also sets the engine's nested concurrency:
`renderMedia` receives `concurrency` from the profile (the measured
tab count), so what the budget accounted for is what the engine
runs. `renderFinal`'s video and poster still acquire sequentially;
`inspectMotion` acquires through the stills it renders.

**Hyperframes** acquires at process granularity: every CLI or helper
process the adapter launches (render, snapshot, check, ffmpeg,
ffprobe) runs inside a reservation whose profile follows the
process (see the table). Sequential launches inside one operation
re-acquire between steps, which keeps the accounting exact at any
instant and lets a contended machine interleave jobs at step
boundaries, a natural checkpoint rhythm. The single exemption is
the runtime probe's `--version` check: a version probe must answer
even when the render budget is fully booked, and it launches no
browser or encoder.

Budget refusals map onto adapter errors at both seams: a cancelled
wait becomes `MG_CANCELLED` (exit class 8); an exhausted budget
becomes a retryable `MG_RENDER_FAILED` (exit class 5) whose message
names the load that refused it.

## Cancellation semantics

- Cancelling a **waiting** acquire aborts the wait promptly; nothing
  launches.
- Cancelling a **running** render is the adapter's engine-level
  cancel (Remotion's cancel signal; Hyperframes child termination).
  The operation then unwinds through its `finally`, which releases
  the reservation: capacity and processes are freed together, and
  the test suite proves both halves (the reservation leaves the
  ledger and the cancelled job's worker processes are gone while a
  competing job completes correctly).
- A **failed** job follows the same path: the error propagates, the
  `finally` releases, and a concurrent job's output is unaffected.
  The failure-isolation case in run-resources asserts the survivor's
  output byte for byte and that only the survivor's reservation
  remains while it finishes.

## Large jobs and the canonical timeline

The scheduler never touches timing. Both adapters already process a
job scene by scene (Hyperframes renders and probes each scene
segment before the next; the MG-021 timeline governs how scenes are
laid out), so a job's reservation is sized for its largest single
segment's peak, not the sum of the job, and intermediate storage is
bounded by the per-operation disk declaration rather than by the
job's total length. Prefer independent scene and chunk work over
redundant full renders: re-render the segment that changed, under
its own reservation, against the same canonical timeline.

## Follow-up: the dispatcher seam (documented, not built)

MG-025 owns `dispatch.ts` in this build wave, so the dispatcher
does not yet know the budget exists. Adapter-level acquisition is
the enforcement point today and is sufficient for every acceptance
behavior: all renders flow through the adapters. The follow-up,
for whoever next owns the dispatcher: surface the budget snapshot
in dispatch outcomes (so a caller can see the reservation its render
held), and consider an optional whole-job reservation for callers
who would rather queue once per job than per operation. Neither
changes the accounting; both are reporting and ergonomics.

## Re-measuring on new hardware

Run `node dist/tests/measure-resources.js --write-evidence` on the
target machine class (or the full `run-resources` suite, whose
measurement case does the same and asserts the shipped profiles
cover what it measured). Update `MEASURED_PEAKS_MB` in
`src/resources/profiles.ts` from the new record's phase peaks,
keeping the margin rule. Profiles from another machine's
documentation are not transferable; that is the point of measuring.
