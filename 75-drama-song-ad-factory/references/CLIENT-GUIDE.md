# Lights, Camera, You! The Step-by-Step Guide to Your Video Dramatization with Music

*Your offer, told as a story, sung as an original song, and delivered as a finished video. You are the Executive Producer. We bring the whole studio.*

---

## Welcome to the Studio, Executive Producer

Congratulations on your new job title. For the next little while, you are the Executive Producer of your very own video dramatization with music. That means you do the fun part: you make the big decisions, you say "yes" or "change that," and you walk the red carpet at the end. Everything else (the writing, the casting, the composing, the filming, the editing) is handled by a whole Hollywood team working for you, all at the same time.

So what exactly are you getting? A video dramatization with music. Think of a short movie that tells the story of your offer, stars characters you choose, features an original song written just for you, and ends with captions and a clear call to action that tells viewers exactly what to do next. It is not a slideshow and it is not a talking head. It is a story people feel, and a song they will hum afterward.

Here is the best part: you are never left guessing. You approve three things before any filming begins (the script, the song, and the storyboard). Nothing important happens behind your back. If you do not love something, you say so, and the crew fixes it.

This guide walks you through the whole production, one step at a time, from "I just heard about this" to "my finished video just arrived." We will use one running example so you can see exactly what good looks like. Meet **Maya**, who runs a small bakery and is launching a cookie-decorating class. You will watch Maya answer every single question, so by the end you will know exactly what to say.

> **Did you know?** Most video projects stall because the first brief is vague. You are about to beat that problem by answering the studio's questions, in the order it asks them, the smart way.

## How This Guide Works

Every step in this guide follows the same four beats, like a good scene:

1. **What happens**: what is going on at this point in the production.
2. **Why it matters**: why this step exists, so nothing feels random.
3. **How to do it**: exactly what to do or say, with examples.
4. **What you'll see**: what appears on your screen, so you always know you are on track.

Steps are in the order you will actually experience them. You can read straight through once, then keep it open as a companion while you answer your questions. Look for these little boxes along the way:

- **Pro tip**: a shortcut or trick from people who have done this before.
- **Did you know?**: a fun fact that makes the process make sense.
- **Great answer / Weak answer**: real examples of what to say, and what to avoid.

# ACT 1: GET READY

## STEP 1: Scout Your Location (The Before-You-Start Checklist)

**What happens.** Before the cameras roll, every good producer does a little prep. This step is a ten-minute scavenger hunt: gather a few things so that when the questions arrive, you can answer quickly and confidently instead of stopping to search for a link or hunt down a logo.

**Why it matters.** The quality of your video is mostly decided by the quality of your answers. When you have your offer link, your audience, your next step and your proof ready, the crew gets a crisp brief and builds exactly what you picture. When you answer in a hurry, you get a guess. Ten minutes now saves a lot of back-and-forth later.

**How to do it.** Work down the checklist below. If you can tick every box, you are ready for Step 2.

**Your before-you-start checklist**

- [ ] Have your offer name and its link ready.
- [ ] Write one line: who the video is for.
- [ ] Write one line: what viewers should do next.
- [ ] Gather your logo, product photos and any proof (a testimonial or a result).
- [ ] If you have saved characters, know their names.
- [ ] Set aside 20 to 30 minutes for the three approvals.

**What Maya did.** Maya opened a note on her phone and typed: "Offer: Sweet Details Cookie-Decorating Class, link: sweetdetailsbakery.example.com/class. For: busy moms who want a creative hobby. Next step: book a seat in the Saturday class. Proof: a photo of last month's students holding their cookies, plus a review that says 'I decorated 12 cookies and cried happy tears.' Logo: the pink whisk." That took her six minutes.

> **Pro tip:** Write your two key lines (who it is for, what they should do) in your phone's notes app now. You will paste them straight into the questions later.

**What you'll see.** Nothing on screen yet. This is your backstage warm-up. When your checklist is ticked, you are ready to move on.

# ACT 2: TELL YOUR STORY

*The questions below come in the order the studio asks them. Each one shapes a different part of your video. Answer them in order, and remember: you can always accept the recommended option and move on.*

## STEP 2: What Are We Promoting?

**What happens.** The first creative question is the foundation of everything: *What product or offer are we promoting, and what link or assets should we use?* This tells the crew what the story, the song and the call to action are about.

**Why it matters.** Every other decision grows from this one. The Screenwriter builds the story around your offer. The Composer writes lyrics that name it. The Editor puts your link on screen at the end. If the offer name or link is wrong, it is wrong everywhere, so we never skip this question. We only follow up if the link is missing or the spelling looks off.

**How to do it.** Give three things: the exact name of your offer, the exact web address, and a mention of the assets you want used (logo, photos, one proof point such as a testimonial). Copy and paste the link rather than typing it from memory.

**Great answer:** "My 6-week Confident Speaker program, https://example.com/speaker. Use my logo and the testimonial PDF." It has the name, the link and the assets.

**Weak answer (and how to fix it):** "My coaching thing." There is no name, no link and no assets. Fix it by adding all three: "My 6-week Confident Speaker program, [link]. Use my logo and the testimonial PDF."

**What Maya said:** "The Sweet Details Cookie-Decorating Class, sweetdetailsbakery.example.com/class. Please use my pink whisk logo, the photo of last month's students, and the review that says 'I decorated 12 cookies and cried happy tears.'"

> **Pro tip:** Proof is your secret weapon. One real result or testimonial makes the story feel true, not salesy.

**What you'll see.** Your offer repeated back to you, name and link, so you can spot a typo immediately.

---

## STEP 3: Who Is It For, and What Should They Do?

**What happens.** Next, the crew needs two things: your ideal viewer and the one action you want them to take. We ask: *Who is this video for, and what should they do after watching it?*

**Why it matters.** A story that speaks to everyone speaks to no one. Knowing exactly who is watching lets the Screenwriter pick the right problem, the right words and the right tone. Knowing the exact next step lets the ending ask for the right thing. One audience plus one action beats ten audiences and five actions every time.

**How to do it.** Describe your best client in one sentence, then name one action: register, book, call or buy. If you already gave one half earlier, we only ask for the missing half. Also say where the action happens (a link, a phone number or a booking page).

**Great answer:** "Women 35-55 who want a second income. Register for my free masterclass." One audience, one action.

**Weak answer (and how to fix it):** "Anybody who might want it. They can do whatever, call, click, email, follow me, buy." Too wide, and the ending would have no punch. Fix it: pick your best client and your single best action.

**What Maya said:** "Busy moms, 30 to 50, who miss having a creative hobby. They should book a seat in the Saturday class at sweetdetailsbakery.example.com/class."

> **Did you know?** Videos with one clear next step tend to work better than videos that offer a menu. Viewers decide in seconds.

**What you'll see.** A short summary line of your audience and your next step.

## STEP 4: Who Stars in Your Video?

**What happens.** Time for casting. We ask: *Do you want to create a new character for this video, or use one you've used before?* If you have characters saved with us, we tell you how many (for example, "You have 3 characters saved"). Option 1 is to create new (recommended). Option 2 and up let you use a saved character by name.

**Why it matters.** Your characters are your actors, and your viewers will spend the whole video with them. The Casting Director makes sure they look exactly the same from the first shot to the last. A saved character is also a great way to build recognition across several videos.

**How to do it.** For a new character, describe their name, age, look and clothing. A reference photo helps if you want a likeness. For a saved character, just give the name from your list. If you accept the recommended choice (create new), we take it from there.

**Great answer:** "1. A warm 40-year-old braider named Tasha with locs, wearing a denim apron." Name, age, look, clothing.

**Weak answer (and how to fix it):** "A woman." We would have to invent everything. Fix it by adding a name and two or three visible details: age, hair, clothing.

**What Maya said:** "1. Create new. Her name is Maya, around 38, curly dark hair in a messy bun, flour on her cheek, a pink apron with a whisk on it. Warm smile, always laughing." (Yes, she cast herself. Producers are allowed.)

> **Pro tip:** Give your character one signature detail, like the pink apron. Viewers remember it, and it keeps the look consistent in every scene.

**What you'll see.** Your character described back to you. Later, in the storyboard, you will see them in every shot.

## STEP 5: What Is This Video For?

These next questions are the card's own, and they come in the order the card asks them, PURPOSE first.

**What happens.** The first card question is what the video is for. You get four choices: grow your business with the full package, tell a client's success story, teach a lesson, or make a video for social media.

**Why it matters.** What the video is for decides its shape, its length and where it goes. One answer tells the crew whether to build a full episode with trailers and ad clips around it, or a single video made to fit one app.

**How to do it.** Pick the option that matches the result you want, not the way you imagine it being made. If you are not sure, take the full package: one production gives you an episode, two trailers that send people to it, and two ready-to-run ad clips.

**Great answer:** "Grow my business." You want the whole package from one production.

**What Maya said:** "Grow my business. My Saturday class needs a steady stream of bookings."

## STEP 6: How Long Is the Full Episode? (Full Package Only)

**What happens.** Chosen "Grow my business" and you are asked how long the full episode should be. The card shows its own length options (60 seconds, 90 seconds, 2 minutes, 3 minutes, 5 minutes and the 10-minute long version), and 3 minutes is recommended for a full episode. Your 60-second and 90-second trailers and ad clips are included.

**Why it matters.** The episode is the story people watch all the way through; the trailers and ad clips are what send people to it. Three minutes is the shortest episode that still earns two good trailers. The longer episodes are made with KIE, not Agnes.

**How to do it.** Pick the length that fits how much story you have. If this is your first one, take 3 minutes.

**Great answer:** "3 minutes." The shortest episode that still earns two good trailers.

**What Maya said:** "3 minutes. I want to see how it performs before I go longer."

## STEP 7: Do You Have Permission? (Success Stories Only)

**What happens.** Told a client's success story, and you are asked one question: do you have your client's permission to use their testimonial? Yes, or no and the order pauses until you have it.

**Why it matters.** A client's own words can only be used when they say so. Without permission nothing is generated and nothing is charged, so your money and your client's trust both stay safe.

**How to do it.** Ask your client first, then answer. If the answer is no, we save your answers exactly as they are and pick the whole thing back up when you have it.

**Great answer:** "Yes." Your client is happy to be quoted.

**What Maya said:** "Yes. She asked me to share it."

## STEP 8: Where Will This Video Run? (Social Videos Only)

**What happens.** Chose a video for social media, and you are asked where it will run: Facebook or Instagram as a paid ad, TikTok, LinkedIn, or a free post.

**Why it matters.** Each app has its own length and shape rules, so the video is made to fit the one you pick rather than resized afterwards and cropped badly.

**How to do it.** Pick the app your audience actually uses. Facebook or Instagram is the widest reach for a vertical video.

**Great answer:** "Facebook or Instagram - paid ad." One vertical video fits Reels, Stories and the feed there.

**What Maya said:** "Facebook or Instagram - paid ad. That is where my students are."

## STEP 9: How Long Should It Run?

**What happens.** Now we pick the running time. We ask: *How long do you want your ad to be?* The card's options are 60 seconds (recommended), 90 seconds, 2 minutes, 3 minutes plus 60- and 90-second clips, 5 minutes plus clips, and 10 minutes plus clips.

**Why it matters.** Length decides the size of the story, the length of the song and the cost. A 60-second video is a single, punchy idea. A 10-minute video is a full mini-movie with room to breathe. Longer videos cost more because there are more shots to film. Length also sets how many storyboard pictures you approve: 8 for a 60-second ad, 24 for a 2-minute ad, then about one more for every five seconds of runtime, capped at 120 pictures for any ad. The 3-, 5- and 10-minute options also come with 60- and 90-second clips for social media, so one big production gives you a stack of shareable pieces.

**How to do it.** Think about where the video will play. Instagram and Reels love 60 seconds. A sales page or webinar warm-up can use 3 to 5 minutes. If you accept 60 seconds, we move straight on.

**Great answer:** "60 seconds. It's for Instagram." The length and the reason.

**Weak answer (and how to fix it):** "As long as it needs." We cannot size the story from that. Fix it by picking one of the card's six options, and mention where it will play.

**What Maya said:** "60 seconds. I'll post it on Instagram and Facebook."

> **Did you know?** Short is not small. A 60-second drama can carry a full emotional arc: problem, turning point, happy ending.

**What you'll see.** Your chosen length on your card, along with the cost for it.

**How to do it.** If most people will watch on a phone, choose Vertical. A paid ad and a free post are always made vertical, so this question only comes up for the other choices.

**Great answer:** "Vertical." Your audience watches on a phone.

## STEP 11: What Does Your Song Feel Like?

**What happens.** The Composer needs a mood. We ask: *Which sound fits your story?* The card lists every sound in the studio's catalog - eighteen in all, with Soul Ballad first and recommended. The three you will hear about most:

- **Soul Ballad**: slow and heartfelt, with a big emotional chorus (recommended).
- **R&B Flow**: rap verses on the beat with a smooth sung hook.
- **Soul Rise**: starts slow and sad, then lifts into an upbeat, hopeful groove.

**Why it matters.** Music is the emotional engine of your video. The same story feels completely different set to a slow ballad than to an upbeat groove. Choosing a style that matches how you want viewers to feel (and who they are) is one of the biggest levers you have.

**How to do it.** Imagine your viewer's emotional journey. If you want tears and warmth, choose Soul Ballad. If you want swagger and rhythm, choose R&B Flow. If your story goes from struggle to victory, choose Soul Rise. If you accept Soul Ballad, we continue. Mentioning one or two songs you like is a nice bonus.

**Great answer:** "Soul Rise. It starts with the problem and ends hopeful." The style and the reason.

**Weak answer (and how to fix it):** "Something good." There is no style here. Fix it by choosing one of the card's eighteen sounds, even if you are unsure. You will hear 3 versions later and can adjust then.

**What Maya said:** "Soul Rise. My moms start out feeling like they have no time for themselves, and I want the end to feel like a celebration."

> **Pro tip:** Match the style to the arc of your story, not just your own taste. Your audience is the one listening.

**What you'll see.** Your chosen style on your card. You will hear it for real at the song approval.

## STEP 17: Approval 1, The Script (Approve the Words)

**What happens.** Scene one of production: the Screenwriter writes your story and the song lyrics, and sends them to you *before* the song is made. We ask you to approve, or tell us what to change.

**Why it matters.** The words are the cheapest and easiest thing to change. Once the song and video are made, changing a word is a lot more work. This is the Producer's sign-off, and it is required.

**How to do it.** Read the lyrics out loud, because you will hear awkward phrases that your eyes skim over. Check the offer name, the link and the call to action. Flag anything you would not say to a client.

**Here is a taste of what Maya received:**

> **Title:** "Sweet Details"
> **Scene 1:** Maya stands in her kitchen at night, plain cookies on the cooling rack, scrolling her phone. She sighs.
> **Verse 1 (sung):** *"Another long day, the dishes in the sink, I used to make things pretty, now I barely think..."*
> **Scene 2:** A friend slides a decorated cookie across the counter. Maya's eyes light up.
> **Chorus (sung):** *"Sweet details, little by little, every swirl, every sprinkle, you can make it, you can make it..."*
> **Final scene:** Maya and other moms hold up gorgeous cookies. Text on screen: "Book your seat in the Saturday class."

**Great answer:** "Approved. Change 'free class' to 'free masterclass'." Clear and specific.

**Weak answer (and how to fix it):** "Make it better." We do not know which part. Fix it by pointing to the line: "In the chorus, replace 'little by little' with 'bit by bit.'"

**What Maya said:** "Love it! Change 'Sweet details' in verse 2 to 'Sweet Details Bakery,' and approved."

> **Pro tip:** Read the script to someone else, even a pet. If any line trips your tongue, say so.

**What you'll see.** Your script and lyrics in a clean message, with a clear "Approve, or tell us what to change."

## STEP 18: Approval 2, The Song (Pick 1 of 3)

**What happens.** Next, the Casting Director creates your characters while the Composer records your original song. You receive **three labelled versions, numbered 1, 2 and 3** (each label is the arrangement's own name, such as Polished Modern or Warm Live Band), and the message asks: *Reply 1, 2 or 3 to pick your song.*

**Why it matters.** The song carries your whole video, so this choice matters. Three versions let you pick the one that fits, rather than hoping the first try was right.

**How to do it.** Listen to all three, on speakers or good headphones. Listen once for the overall feeling, then once for the words. Check that your offer name is sung clearly and spelled right. Choose with your audience in mind, not only your own taste.

**What Maya received:**

> **1 - Polished Modern:** Slower, piano-led, with a gentle build.
> **2 - Warm Live Band:** A warm start that lifts into a bright, clap-along chorus.
> **3 - Cinematic Strings:** More upbeat from the first note, with a bouncy rhythm.

**Great answer:** "2. I can't stop humming the chorus." Choice and reason.

**Weak answer (and how to fix it):** "They're all good." We need one. Fix it by choosing the one you would play on a long drive, then add a note if any word needs fixing.

**What Maya said:** "2! And can the singer say 'Sweet Details Bakery' a little slower?"

> **Did you know?** If you cannot decide, play all three for someone in your target audience and watch which one makes them move.

**What you'll see.** Three numbered audio files, 1, 2 and 3, each with its label, plus a question asking you to reply with one number.

**[INFOGRAPHIC: do-and-don't]** *Title: "How to Judge the Song."* Do: listen on speakers or headphones; listen twice (feeling, then words); check the offer name; think of your audience. Don't: decide from phone speakers in a noisy room; choose only by your own taste; skip the spelling check.

## STEP 19: Approval 3, The Storyboard (Approve Every Shot)

**What happens.** Now the Storyboard Artists draw every shot, with a written card and a picture for each one. How many pictures that is depends on the length you picked: a 60-second ad gets 8 storyboard pictures, a 2-minute ad gets 24, and after that the count grows by about one picture for every five seconds of runtime, up to a maximum of 120 pictures for any ad. We ask you to approve, or tell us what to change. This is the very last stop before any video is filmed.

**Why it matters.** The storyboard is your last chance to change what a shot looks like cheaply. It shows who is in each shot, what they are doing, where, and what is on screen. Once filming starts, shots are expensive to redo. This is the safest moment to say, "Show the storefront here."

**How to do it.** Plan on about ten minutes. Go shot by shot. Check that your product, logo and characters appear the way you want, and that the call to action lands at the end.

**A signature opening you can ask for.**

Your video can open with a sweeping drone fly-in.

**What one shot from Maya's storyboard looked like:**

> **Shot 4 of 10**
> **Picture:** Maya, in her pink apron, pipes a pink swirl onto a cookie, with a warm, painted kitchen behind her.
> **Card:** Maya smiles as she decorates. Music: chorus begins. On-screen text: none.
> **Camera:** Close, gentle push-in.

**Great answer:** "Approved. Change shot 4 to show the storefront."

**Weak answer (and how to fix it):** "Something feels off." We cannot find it. Fix it by naming the shot number and what to change.

**What Maya said:** "Approved! Shot 7, can you add my logo on the cookie box?"

> **Pro tip:** Count how many times your logo and offer name appear. It should be at least once at the end, and ideally once earlier too.

**What you'll see.** A grid of numbered shots, each with a picture and a short card.

**[INFOGRAPHIC: flow diagram]** *Title: "Anatomy of a Storyboard Shot."* Data: one frame with four callouts: "Picture" (what it looks like), "Card" (what happens), "Sound" (which part of the song plays), "Text" (what is on screen).

# ACT 4: SHOW TIME

## STEP 20: While You Wait, the Crew Gets to Work

**What happens.** You have pressed all three gold stars. Now you can put your feet up. Many crews film different scenes at the same time, so every shot is made in parallel. That is why it is fast. Your characters are lip-synced, the Editor cuts everything together with captions and your call to action, and the Screening Room runs quality checks.

**Why it matters.** This is where the magic multiplies. Instead of one person doing one thing after another, a whole Hollywood team works for you at once. And because every shot was approved first, the crew knows exactly what to make.

**Our special lip-sync technology.** Your characters do not just appear in your video. They sing your song, and every line lands on the beat.

- **Real emotion, not a puppet:** faces stay expressive and true to each character from the first note to the last.
- **Matched to your song:** the singing follows the exact version of the song you picked.
- **Checked before you see it:** every singing shot is reviewed. Each segment gets at most two paid tries; a second try happens only when a person marks a visible defect and the input changes, and then the best-measured take is kept. A sung line the checker cannot confirm is kept and flagged, and you are shown its mouth strip - there is never an automatic paid redo.

**The Screening Room.** Every shot and the final cut are reviewed. Anything off is caught before it reaches you, and the card's planning allowance covers that work. No rough drafts land on your desk.

**How to do it.** Nothing! Just watch for the delivery message. If a message arrives from the crew, answer it, because the crew might need one quick decision.

**What Maya did.** She went to bake a batch of cookies, which is exactly what a Producer should do.

**What you'll see.** Progress updates, then a message that your video is ready.

**[INFOGRAPHIC: timeline]** *Title: "Behind the Scenes."* Data (in order): Script approved; Characters created; Song chosen; Storyboard approved; Shots filmed at the same time; Lip-sync; Edit with captions and call to action; Quality checks; Final video. Draw it as a single film strip with the filming stage drawn as five parallel tracks.

## STEP 21: The Premiere (Delivery and Clips)

**What happens.** Drum roll. Your final video is delivered, edited, captioned, sung, and ending with your call to action. If you chose a 3-, 5- or 10-minute video, you also get 60- and 90-second clips for social media. Those are your trailers, ready to share.

**Why it matters.** One big production becomes a whole content pack. The full video can live on your sales page, while the short clips travel on social media and send people back to the full story.

**How to do it.** Watch the full video once, start to finish, as a viewer. Then watch again with your Producer hat on. Share it where your audience is. Post a clip, link to the offer, and watch your viewers take the next step.

**What Maya did.** She watched it three times, cried happy tears (just like her student's review), and posted it on Instagram that evening with the booking link. Her Saturday class filled up by the weekend.

**What you'll see.** Your finished video, plus (for 3-, 5- and 10-minute videos) your 60- and 90-second clips.

> **Did you know?** Trailers are not just "cuts." They are made to stand alone, so viewers who see only the clip still know exactly what to do next.

**[INFOGRAPHIC: checklist]** *Title: "Premiere Night Checklist."* Data: Watch the full video once as a viewer; Watch again as the Producer; Post your best clip with the link; Put the full video on your offer page; Tell the crew what you loved (so the next one is even better).
