# Opening proposals — worked candidate sets

Status: teaching material, both distributions (Skill 75 / 999 twin).
Rule source: `references/sensational-openings.md`. Every candidate below
carries all six required fields; each set holds exactly three genuinely
different candidates and names the recommended one.

All characters and events here are composite fiction. Nothing in this file is
a real customer story, a measured result, or a claim about any business.
Planning labels: `COMPOSITE`, `FICTIONAL_PLOT_EVENT`.

---

## Set A — credit repair (audience: denied a loan they needed)

### A1 — revelation at the bank desk

- **opening_line:** "She deleted the file that proved I paid."
- **headline:** THE DEBT I PAID KEPT ME FROM THE HOUSE
- **first_action:** A loan officer slides the denial across the desk and turns it face down before I can read line four.
- **question:** What did she delete, and why was it mine to prove?
- **why_this_audience:** This audience already suspects the record is wrong and that nobody will believe them. Hearing someone else say it out loud is relief before it is fear.
- **offer_link:** A credit repair offer exists precisely to obtain, dispute and correct records held by bureaus and furnishers. The deleted file is the same object the offer works on: the file, not the debt.

### A2 — humiliation in the driveway

- **opening_line:** "Everything I owned was on the lawn by nine, and the woman taking pictures was the one who sold me the loan."
- **headline:** THE LOAN THAT TOOK THE HOUSE
- **first_action:** A couch leg snaps as it lands in wet grass; a neighbour's porch light snaps on.
- **question:** Who approved that loan, and who is photographing my loss?
- **why_this_audience:** The audience fears being seen failing more than the failure itself. Public shame is the sharpest edge available to them.
- **offer_link:** The photographed loan is a credit decision made on a report. Repairing the report is the mechanism that changes future decisions — the crisis and the service meet on the same document.

### A3 — confrontation on the phone

- **opening_line:** "I paid them every month for six years, and this morning they told me I don't exist in their system."
- **headline:** SIX YEARS OF PAYMENTS. NO RECORD.
- **first_action:** She reads her account number into the phone; the typing on the other end stops.
- **question:** Where did six years go?
- **why_this_audience:** Being erased is worse than being refused, and this audience has been told "we have no record of that" before.
- **offer_link:** Disputing and rebuilding a credit file is exactly the work of re-establishing a record. The offer's first job is the crisis: making the file say what happened.

**Scores (shock / curiosity / relevance)**

| Candidate | Shock | Curiosity | Relevance | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A1 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 13 |
| A2 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 12 |
| A3 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 15 |

**Recommended: A3.** Highest on all three. "I don't exist in their system" is
the strongest available combination of erasure (shock), an unanswered
where-did-it-go (curiosity), and a file that the offer literally rebuilds
(relevance). A2 has the biggest single image but a longer causal walk to the
offer; A1 is strong and is the safer alternative if the client objects to a
phone-call scene.

**Rejected tame version of this set:** "I had some credit trouble a while back"
/ "My credit wasn't great, honestly" / "Things got a little difficult
financially." One candidate in three costumes, no consequence, no question.

## Set B — business coaching (audience: owner with payroll due)

### B1 — catastrophe at payroll

- **opening_line:** "I paid nine people out of a credit card at 11:58 at night, and the card declined."
- **headline:** PAYROLL WAS DUE IN TWO MINUTES
- **first_action:** A thumb taps "send" on a payroll app; the red banner lands before the thumb lifts.
- **question:** What happens to those nine people on Monday?
- **why_this_audience:** Every owner in this audience has done the mental arithmetic of payroll against a card limit. The time stamp makes it their own Friday.
- **offer_link:** The offer teaches the cash-conversion and pricing decisions that produce margin before payroll, so the card is not the payroll account. The crisis is a margin failure, which is the offer's subject.

### B2 — humiliation at the client dinner

- **opening_line:** "My biggest client took my whole team to dinner, and the cancellation email arrived while we were ordering dessert."
- **headline:** THE CONTRACT ENDED OVER DESSERT
- **first_action:** A phone face-up on the table lights with the subject line; the client's hand covers it too late.
- **question:** What did I do that made me replaceable in one quarter?
- **why_this_audience:** Owners fear being liked and not needed. This is the exact fear, staged in public.
- **offer_link:** The offer's work is positioning and offer design — the reason a client stays for a reason other than affection. The dinner is the diagnosis; the curriculum is the treatment.

### B3 — discovery of a betrayal

- **opening_line:** "The company filing had my partner's signature on it, and my name nowhere."
- **headline:** HE BUILT IT BESIDE ME
- **first_action:** A printed filing is laid on a kitchen table; a finger stops on a signature that is not hers.
- **question:** How long has this company existed?
- **why_this_audience:** This audience has a partner, a co-founder or a family member in the business, and the fear is structural, not financial.
- **offer_link:** The offer covers ownership, agreements and decision rights — the controls that would have made this discovery early and cheap. The filing is the symptom; governance is the mechanism.

**Scores**

| Candidate | Shock | Curiosity | Relevance | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| B1 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 14 |
| B2 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 12 |
| B3 | 5 | 5 | 3 | 13 |

**Recommended: B1.** It wins on relevance without giving up shock: a declining
card at 11:58 p.m. is a physical event with a deadline, and the offer's subject
is the number that caused it. B3 has the highest curiosity and is the better
pick only if the client's offer is specifically about partnership or ownership
structure, where its relevance score rises to 5.

**Rejected tame version:** "Running a business is harder than it looks." No
person, no moment, no stake — an ad for the category, not for this audience.

## Set C — dental practice (audience: adults hiding their teeth)

### C1 — confrontation over the X-ray

- **opening_line:** "The dentist stopped talking, turned the screen around, and asked me how long I'd known."
- **headline:** HOW LONG HAVE YOU KNOWN?
- **first_action:** A gloved hand rotates a monitor toward the chair; the patient's reflection sits in the black edge of the screen.
- **question:** Known what — and how bad is it?
- **why_this_audience:** This audience has postponed a visit specifically to avoid being told off. The line puts the judgement in the room before the diagnosis.
- **offer_link:** The offer is the exam and the treatment plan that stops the damage and the shame cycle. The X-ray is the offer's own first artifact; the opening is the appointment they have been avoiding.

### C2 — humiliation before an interview

- **opening_line:** "I practised the smile in the car mirror, and then I covered my mouth for the whole interview."
- **headline:** SHE NEVER SHOWED HER TEETH
- **first_action:** In a sun visor mirror, a woman rehearses a smile; her hand drops over her mouth as the interviewer's door opens.
- **question:** What did she decide about herself in that mirror?
- **why_this_audience:** The audience is not afraid of pain — they are afraid of being seen. This is the exact rehearsal they have performed.
- **offer_link:** Cosmetic and restorative treatment changes the thing the mirror showed. The offer's outcome is the hand coming down.

### C3 — catastrophe by cost

- **opening_line:** "The infection that started in a tooth ended up costing me four days in hospital and a bill I still can't open."
- **headline:** THE CLEANING THAT BECAME A HOSPITAL BILL
- **first_action:** An emergency-room wristband is cut off and dropped beside an unopened statement.
- **question:** What would the cleaning have cost?
- **why_this_audience:** Money is the stated reason this audience postpones, so the cost of waiting is the argument that reaches them.
- **offer_link:** Preventive exams and cleanings are the offer's entry service; the hospital bill is the price of skipping them. The mechanism is direct and arithmetic.

**Scores**

| Candidate | Shock | Curiosity | Relevance | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| C1 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 15 |
| C2 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 12 |
| C3 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 14 |

**Recommended: C1.** One sentence carries dread, a question and the practice's
own artifact in the same breath, and it makes the viewer the patient. C3 is the
strongest runner-up and is the safer choice where the client will not show a
hospital scene; C2 is the warmest and the weakest on shock.

**Rejected tame version:** "A healthy smile changes everything." A slogan, not
an opening — nobody is in a room, nothing has happened, and the question is
missing.

## Set D — roofing / storm repair (audience: homeowner after a storm)

### D1 — discovery of the same man, two names

- **opening_line:** "The contractor I paid in March was standing in my kitchen, using a different name for my neighbour."
- **headline:** SAME TRUCK. DIFFERENT NAME.
- **first_action:** Through a kitchen window, a magnetic sign is peeled off a truck door and replaced with another.
- **question:** Who did I actually pay?
- **why_this_audience:** This audience has already been pitched by three crews in one week and cannot tell them apart. The fear is being fooled by a professional.
- **offer_link:** The offer is licensed, insured, permit-pulling work with a paper trail. The crisis is the absence of exactly those three things.

### D2 — catastrophe in the nursery

- **opening_line:** "The tarp was never put up, and at two in the morning the ceiling came down over the crib."
- **headline:** THE ROOFER TOOK THE DEPOSIT AND LEFT
- **first_action:** Plaster and soaked insulation fall into a lit nursery; a mobile of paper stars spins and stops.
- **question:** Who do you call at two in the morning after the crew disappears?
- **why_this_audience:** Storm-damaged homeowners are targeted by deposit-then-vanish crews, and this is the worst version of the outcome they already fear.
- **offer_link:** The offer is an insured contractor that finishes and answers the phone. The missing tarp is the specific failure the offer's process prevents.

### D3 — reversal at the permit office

- **opening_line:** "The permit was never filed, the crew was never licensed, and the inspection was signed by a man who does not work for the city."
- **headline:** NO PERMIT. NO LICENCE. NO INSPECTOR.
- **first_action:** A clerk slides a printout across the counter with three blank fields; the homeowner's finger stops on the second blank.
- **question:** What is holding up my roof right now?
- **why_this_audience:** This audience has been told "it's all taken care of" and has no way to check. Here is a person who checked.
- **offer_link:** The offer's differentiator is the paperwork and the inspection. The crisis is the missing paperwork, which is the offer's own proof asset.

**Scores**

| Candidate | Shock | Curiosity | Relevance | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| D1 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 14 |
| D2 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 14 |
| D3 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 14 |

**Recommended: D2.** Tied on the total, so the tie-break is the image: a
ceiling falling over a crib is the only one of the three that cannot be
explained away as a misunderstanding, and it lands the offer's phone-answering
promise in the same frame. D1 is the better pick where the client's differentiator
is vetting rather than response; D3 where it is permitting.

**Rejected tame version:** "After the storm, many homeowners don't know where to
turn." A narrator describing the audience instead of a person living in it.

## Set E — tax relief (audience: taxpayer with unopened envelopes)

The crises in this set are invented on purpose: a composite character may lose
the year, the account and the pay stub (`FICTIONAL_PLOT_EVENT`, `COMPOSITE`,
§7.1 of `sensational-openings.md`). What is never invented is a claim about the
real offer — that boundary is §7.2 and it does not move.

### E1 — reversal on the speaker phone

- **opening_line:** "I paid them four hundred a month for a year, and the woman on the phone just told me my case was never filed."
- **headline:** A YEAR OF PAYMENTS. NO CASE FILED.
- **first_action:** A shoebox of stamped receipts is emptied onto a kitchen table; the phone lies on speaker between them.
- **question:** If nothing was filed, what was I paying for?
- **why_this_audience:** This audience has already paid one company to fix it and is now being asked to trust another. The fear is being fooled twice, in the same way, by the same kind of promise.
- **offer_link:** The offer is the filing itself — forms submitted to the agency, with copies the client keeps. The crisis is the missing filing; the offer sells exactly the artifact that was missing, not a promise around it.

### E2 — catastrophe at the register

- **opening_line:** "Forty-three dollars of groceries, and the card declined — the levy had taken the account before breakfast."
- **headline:** THE ACCOUNT WAS EMPTY BEFORE BREAKFAST
- **first_action:** A debit card taps twice at a register; the cashier stops bagging and looks up at her.
- **question:** How does this family eat this week?
- **why_this_audience:** This audience does the balance arithmetic before every small purchase. The fear is not the big loss — it is the ordinary purchase failing in public.
- **offer_link:** A levy lifts when the case is resolved or a hold is filed, and both are the offer's actual work. The frozen account is the enforcement arriving; the offer is the paperwork that stops it.

### E3 — humiliation at the pay counter

- **opening_line:** "A third of my check was gone before I ever touched it, and the foreman read the new line out loud before I could fold the stub."
- **headline:** GARNISHED BEFORE IT REACHED ME
- **first_action:** A pay stub slides across a shop counter; a coworker's finger lands on the new deduction line.
- **question:** What did the crew just learn about me?
- **why_this_audience:** This audience fears the debt becoming public at work more than the debt itself. The pay stub is the one document they cannot hide from the people who see them every day.
- **offer_link:** Ending a garnishment is filings and negotiations, which is what the offer does. The deduction line is where the case stands today; the offer's outcome is that line gone on the next stub.

**Scores (shock / curiosity / relevance)**

| Candidate | Shock | Curiosity | Relevance | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| E1 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 15 |
| E2 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 14 |
| E3 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 12 |

**Recommended: E1.** The only candidate of the three where the offer is the
missing artifact itself: "never filed" erases a paid year (shock), the money's
whereabouts is a question nobody answers (curiosity), and the service is the
filing the client has been paying for and not receiving (relevance). E2 is the
pick where the client wants a family scene and the differentiator is how fast a
levy releases; E3 where the audience is hourly and the fear is public.

**Rejected tame versions of this set:** "I owed some taxes and it kept me up at
night" / "Tax debt can happen to anyone" / "Don't let tax problems stress your
family." One tame worry in three costumes: no moment, no object, no consequence,
no question. A set like this is not three candidates; it is §8 row 7 of
`sensational-openings.md` wearing different clothes.

## How to use these sets

1. Score your own three candidates with the same numbers before reading the
   recommendation, then compare.
2. If your strongest candidate is not the recommended one, the difference is
   either a veto (name it) or a real difference in the offer — say which.
3. Never submit a set where all three share a crisis type, even if the client
   asked for "a few options." Options with one shape are one option.
4. Every invented event in your set carries `FICTIONAL_PLOT_EVENT` /
   `COMPOSITE` into the beat sheet; no invented event is ever presented as a
   verified fact about a real person or product.
