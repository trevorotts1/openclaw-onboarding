THE PURPLE BILLBOARD PLAYBOOK

How To Use This Pack

The Purple Billboard Playbook runs on a strategy called Hook and Reveal: the billboard post is the hook, the one that stops the scroll and makes the promise, and the seven comments under it are the reveal, the part that pays the promise off.

Every hook in this pack is a Purple Billboard: a Facebook text post on a solid purple background, big bold white text, nothing else on the post. The post makes one numbered promise, always 6 items in this pack. The value is NOT in the post. The value lands in the comments, posted by you, one item per comment, in order, the moment the post goes live: 6 reveal comments, then the Bonus comment last and unnumbered, 7 authored comments in all and never more than 8.

For each hook you get four things: the hook itself, the comment reveal template that fulfills the promise (fill the brackets with your own facts), the named call to action (it goes in the Bonus comment and in your replies and DMs), and the standing Bonus comment template with the two Convert and Flow door placeholders.

RULES THAT NEVER BREAK

Rules that never break: write the full reveal BEFORE you post. Never put a link in the post body. Deliver the exact number you promised: 6 items, 6 reveal comments, Bonus comment last and unnumbered, and never more than 8 authored comments on a post. The Bonus carries a maximum of 2 links. Log every pack you build so you never repeat a hook, topic, or promise, and never recycle an old keyword or link for a different giveaway: old keywords and links stay tied to their original promise. Prepare 30 days of billboard packs at a time for one monthly approval, and post exactly 1 a day; the packs you do not post bank as spares. Rotate the trigger across packs: BREAKING, STOP, numbered promise, and question hooks.

THE PURPLE BILLBOARD PLAYBOOK

The DM Flow And Follow-Up Standard For Every Pack

Personal posts send people to the business Messenger conversation and name the keyword. Deliver the promised item first, inside that conversation. Never assume a tagged link identifies the offer: that is unproven, and the typed keyword is what the conversation works from. If the promised item is unknown, use this line, word for word:

USE THIS LINE, WORD FOR WORD

Hey! I’m looking forward to sharing what I promised you. What keyword did you see in the post?

Mobile comes first for the one-time access pass. Email is asked exactly once, by SMS, with the pass. All other email asks stay in the original Messenger or Instagram thread. Follow-up for missing contact information runs on one shared 3-reminder sequence at 2 hours, 8 hours, and 20 hours, only while the channel permits messaging. Stop after a refusal, after capture, or after the final unanswered message. People who decline still get the promise. Delivery consent and marketing consent are separate.

The final reminder, word for word:

THE FINAL REMINDER, WORD FOR WORD

Let me be direct: do you want these guides, or should I close this out? I offered because I want to help you move forward, but I’m not going to keep asking. If you want access, send me your mobile number and I’ll text your pass. If I don’t hear back, I’ll leave it here. This is my final message about getting you access.
